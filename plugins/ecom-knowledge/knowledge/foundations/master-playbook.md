# Ecom Brain master playbook

## One business, two coordinates

Never classify DTC, POD, dropshipping, and subscription as peers. Start with [[two-axis-taxonomy]]:

1. How is the offer produced and fulfilled?
2. Through which channels is it sold, and who owns the customer relationship?

A handmade seller can be `handmade × marketplace seller + DTC`. A subscription box can be `kitting × DTC + social commerce`. The coordinate is more useful than one label.

## The operating lens

Analyze the merchant in this order:

1. **Offer:** physical, digital, access, service, or temporary use.
2. **Supply:** made, sourced, assembled, licensed, consigned, or rented.
3. **Demand:** owned audience, paid acquisition, marketplace search, retailer relationships, creators, stores, or agents.
4. **Transaction control:** who sets price, checkout, payment terms, returns, and policy.
5. **Economics:** gross margin is insufficient; include acquisition, fulfillment, returns, working capital, payment timing, and repeat behavior.
6. **Operational constraint:** inventory, production capacity, catalog quality, churn, reverse logistics, trust and safety, creator operations, or protocol readiness.
7. **Defensibility:** brand, unique supply, proprietary audience, workflow data, network effects, switching costs, or none yet.

Use [[model-card-method]] so comparisons stay symmetric.

## What changes by channel

- Owned channels such as [[dtc]] improve customer access but require demand generation.
- [[marketplace-seller]] and [[social-live-commerce]] provide discovery while limiting customer ownership and increasing policy dependence.
- [[b2b-wholesale]] trades consumer acquisition for account sales, terms, catalogs, and replenishment.
- [[retail-omnichannel]] adds store inventory and cross-channel execution.
- [[creator-affiliate-commerce]] rents trust and audience from creators; attribution and relationship quality become core operations.
- [[b2b2c-marketplace-operator]] must create liquidity and manage trust on both sides.
- [[agentic-commerce]] may insert a new discovery and checkout layer between merchant and customer.

## What changes by offer and fulfillment

- [[handmade-craft]] has differentiation but capacity and labor-cost constraints.
- [[digital-products]] removes physical fulfillment but concentrates risk in positioning, delivery, licensing, tax, and unauthorized sharing.
- [[subscription-box-kitting]] creates repeat potential while adding procurement, assembly, churn, payment recovery, and cut-off discipline.

## Avada opportunity lens

Prefer problems that recur across multiple coordinates and can be solved through software or data:

- cross-channel catalog and inventory truth;
- attribution and creator/affiliate integrity;
- retention, churn, dunning, and customer lifecycle;
- B2B catalogs, MOQ, terms, quotes, and replenishment;
- returns and reverse logistics;
- merchant readiness for AI discovery and checkout.

Do not convert every content gap into a product gap. Require a recurring workflow, a clear owner, measurable pain, and evidence that merchants pay or lose money today.
