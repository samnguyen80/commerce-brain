# Direct to consumer

## Model

The brand sells directly through an owned storefront or relationship rather than relying on a retailer for the transaction.

## Economics and operations

- The merchant controls price, offer, checkout, merchandising, and much of the customer data.
- It also carries demand-generation, conversion, fulfillment, returns, service, and retention costs.
- Revenue quality depends on contribution margin and repeat behavior, not topline or ROAS alone.

## Growth and defensibility

Brand, owned audience, product differentiation, customer data, merchandising, and retention form the core loop. DTC can coexist with marketplaces, wholesale, retail, creators, and social channels.

## Failure modes

Paid-acquisition dependence, channel attribution confusion, optimizing conversion while margin falls, weak post-purchase experience, and treating DTC as a fulfillment model.

## Avada opportunities

Conversion, personalization, lifecycle messaging, attribution, merchandising, retention, support automation, and unified cross-channel customer data.
