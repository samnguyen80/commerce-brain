All right.
First tip is manage the people and not the position.
Hire for the right people and not the position.
In Jim Collins' book, Good to Great, he talks a lot about having the right people on the bus and always being able to rearrange the seats on the bus.
And so when you're thinking about the needs of your business and you're writing a job description where you're going to start advertising to hire someone, don't get caught up in the details of all the things that that person must do.
Instead, look for the right person who's trainable and coachable and maybe can morph into a couple of unexpected positions in your company.
So you may hire two part-time people that are awesome and have the right skill sets to fill one full-time position.
Don't be set on the position.
Be set on the right people.
You can always rearrange them later.
All right.
Tip number two is be clear with your expectations from the start.
One of the number one reasons we run into employee issues that often lead to firing or letting go of an employee is unmet expectations.
And that comes back on us, the employer, because most often we weren't clear with what the expectations were up front.
So it's kind of difficult for someone to manage and meet those expectations if they don't know what they are.
So it's about being a leader and not always being a best friend.
It's about setting where the boundaries are in your business and what your expectations are so people can live up to them.
If you think, you know what, I don't want to micromanage people.
I'm just going to let them do their thing.
They will fail you every single time because they don't know where the ceiling really is and where they need to strive to reach the next level.
All right.
Third tip, train, train, train.
You are the coach of your team.
And there is no successful athlete that does not get where they're going without having a coach.
It's the fourth quarter.
It is crunch time.
It comes down to not who the best players are on the court, but who is the best coach with the most strategy.
So you can never get enough training with your team, whether that's in the onboarding process or week to week with your ongoing meetings with your entire team.
