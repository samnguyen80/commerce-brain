the amount of revenue that you sold in this particular period of time divided by the number of
orders or transactions that you had in the same period of time to understand what is the average
value per order. Because the cool part about this acronym is the more traffic that you're able to

because it's going to take a lot of work or a lot less work getting to that real end financial goal
that you have in your business. So again, revenue divided by orders in the same period of time as your
average order value. And keep in mind, your average order value in store is probably going to be a little
bit different than your average order value online. So measure them separately. Fourth acronym to know is
UPT units per transaction. This is the total items that you sold in a period of time divided by the
number of transactions that you had in that same period of time. This is important because we're not
in the business of selling one thing, right? We're in the business of selling multiple things. So if
you're in gift, this is bundles and all kinds of little add-ons and upsells. If you're in apparel,

financial goal. All right. Finally, the last acronym I want you to know is your ROAS, return on ad spend.
For those of you running ads, this is critical. Now there are lots of different ways people look
at return on ad spend, but I'm going to give you this very important advice. Your return on ad spend
is the revenue that is associated with the ad that you're running divided by the cost of the campaign,
right? So let's say that you had a $600 attribution on a particular ad. It costs you $300 to run that
campaign. You have a two to one ROAS, and that's really important. What is really critical when you
are measuring your return on ad spend is two things. Number one, if you hire an agency, agency fees have
to be included in your ROAS, because if you have an agency fee that is greater than the investment you're
making, and let's say you have a two to one ROAS, it really kind of cancels itself out. Like you're
probably only breaking even, or in some cases we've seen people actually be money behind when they didn't
consider agency fees in their ROAS. The second thing I would encourage you to think about is making sure
the attribution to each ad is correct. If you have a wide open attribution and you're counting all traffic,
all views, all sales on your website to be attributed to that campaign, your ROAS is going
to be completely jacked up because that means the Facebook live you did, the email you sent, the
organic social media you did is all being attributed to the success of the campaign. So making sure your
attribution window is nailed down and you're using UTM codes or something that can really specifically
allow you to track performance by performance ad spend and attribution, that is really important.
