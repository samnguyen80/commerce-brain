Would you like to make more sales? Of course, everybody would. Today, let's talk about a
psychological movement that can actually help you increase sales, either your retail location or
your wholesale brand. Have you ever heard the word reciprocity? Reciprocity is a psychological
principle where it's a powerful persuasion and it triggers an increase in sales. So how does
reciprocity work? Reciprocity is simple. It's kind of the golden rule, right? If I give you something,
you'll give me something back. If I'm kind to you, you'll extend kindness to me. Reciprocity can really
be helpful in marketing and sales because it does trigger an increase. It creates a bond between you
and the customer where they feel indebted to give you something back because you gave them something
first. So let's talk about how to build that kind of a value stack where your customer will feel more
connected and more trusting and more willing to return to buy from you in the future. All right,
so how can you use reciprocity in this psychological movement to increase sales in your business?
First one's pretty easy. Offer small free gifts. So I see this work at market all the time. I'll go
into a booth and watch a brand interact with a retailer. They'll offer them something simple like a
drink or maybe a snack. The customer happily accepts. Right when you offer something to someone,
then they feel that they have to give something back. And usually the thing they're going to give
back is their time. You've probably seen this even at restaurants, right? Where they offer you free
mints. That's an example of how unexpected generosity can drive stronger customer responses
and improve conversion. So try it. Offer someone something simple when you first see them or greet
them, whether it's in your store, in your booth, or in your showroom. It can be a drink, a snack. It could
be a starburst. But when you give them something, they're going to feel indebted to give their time.
And in that time is going to give you an opportunity to make that sales pitch. Another thing that you can
give them is solving their problems. So beyond something physical, beyond that free gift, what if the thing
you're giving them is knowledge? So service businesses, brands, even retailers, you can do this. You can
help customers before you're even at the point of making a sale. So this could be in the form of a
free consultation, right? Let me do your colors or let's find out what silhouettes or shapes fit best
on your body. You could do an audit. You could give a sample. You could offer some kind of diagnostic
tool, but it's all to create goodwill and position yourself as a helper. I've seen this done on social
media all of the time, right? Here's some tips to help you look better in your clothes. Here's some
tips to help you move your product quicker in store. When you can solve a problem first for someone,
before you even offer them a product to sell, you are building a relationship with that customer.
All right. Creating high value content. This is kind of what I alluded to in the past point about
what you can do on social media. So instead of just giving something physical away, you can provide
educational content, guides, tutorials, resources that feel premium. This kind of valuable free
information builds your credibility and keeps you and your business top of mind. I've seen some brands
do this really well with style guides. They pull out trend reports. Some of my favorite boutiques are
doing things on what to wear to the rodeo, how to dress your family for family photos. This kind of
high value content is creating that rule of reciprocity. You're giving them something they need,
and in return, they're going to give you their time and attention. And that time and attention can
definitely and eventually be moved into a sale. I'd even look at partnering with generous creators.
So there's influencers out there that are helping transfer positive emotions to brands all the
time. And when I say brands, I could be talking about a wholesale brand or retailer. Audiences respond
more positively to campaigns that feel generous rather than transactional. So think about those
influencers when they're, hey girl, I've got you. You need to hide, you know, your muffin top. Let me show

feeling. Have a creator help you with it and use that content to create that reciprocity feeling.
I'm giving you something. What are you giving back? Influencers do this really well with giveaways or
helping their audience. Position yourself the same way. What about this one? Adding an unexpected bonus
to an order. I always love when I get, you know, order something online and I open it up and there's an
unexpected. Maybe a product sample or a card. A surprise inside of a package can increase customer
loyalty significantly. So think about small add-ons. You don't even have to give a product. It could be a
handwritten note or a bonus sample. Maybe it's a coupon for a future purchase. But this creates an
emotional impact far beyond the actual cost. Unexpected bonuses are big winners. Finally, when it comes to
the rule of reciprocity, focus on relationship building. Reciprocity works best when it feels
authentic. The goal is not manipulation. Although this is a psychological tactic, we're not trying
to manipulate people. We're trying to actually and genuinely create positive experiences that are
strengthening that long-term customer relationship. In business, you will be more successful in the long
term if you focus on relationships over transactions. So what's your big takeaway from here?
Giving creates momentum in business. When customers receive unexpected value first,
they are far more likely to trust your brand, remember your business, and eventually become
