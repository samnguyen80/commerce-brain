 Hello OmniTalk Fans! 

 Everyone at this conference is talking about AI. Not everyone knows what&#8217;s actually working.

 I sat down with Nicolas de Bellefonds, Managing Director of BCG X for EMEA and South America, live from the CGF Leadership Studio at the Consumer Goods Forum Global Summit 2026 in Vienna, Austria. Nico has been building AI capabilities since before most companies knew they needed them, and he came armed with fresh research and zero patience for hype. This one is required reading.

 Here are my biggest takeaways:

 The CPG and Retail AI Gap Is Bigger Than You Think BCG&#8217;s latest research put some real numbers behind what a lot of people have been sensing. In CPG, 75% of companies are still stuck in pilot mode, with only 18% having achieved any kind of scaled impact. Retail tells a completely different story: half of retailers have moved to scale, while the other half have done essentially nothing. Feast or famine, as Nico put it. There is no middle ground forming here.

 Where the Value Actually Lives On the CPG side, the biggest gains right now are coming from revenue management, pricing, and increasingly marketing and innovation workflows. On the retail side, supply chain has been the early win, but the real prize Nico sees coming is merchandising, getting the right products at the right price on shelf and negotiating better with suppliers. Both industries are moving toward their core engines, which is exactly where the leverage is.

 Agentic Commerce Is a New Channel, Not Just a New Feature This was one of the more forward-looking parts of the conversation. Nico made clear that the rise of agent-driven e-commerce isn&#8217;t just a UX upgrade. It&#8217;s an entirely new channel that brands and retailers need to master. His advice: start building your own AI agent capability now, not because the ecosystem is ready, but because the proprietary data and consumer behavior insights you&#8217;ll accumulate in the process are irreplaceable. When the protocols catch up, you want to already be in the game, not scrambling to join it.

 If Your AI Roadmap Has 200 Use Cases, Walk Away Nico&#8217;s framework for moving from pilot to scale comes down to two traps to avoid. The first is dispersion, spreading investment and attention across too many use cases at once. The second is incrementalism, using AI to slightly improve how you already operate rather than fundamentally changing how work gets done. Real transformation means accepting that roles will change, skills will change, and the entire organizational construct will need to shift. That&#8217;s not a technology problem. It&#8217;s a managerial one.

 Set Stretch Goals Because the Technology Won&#8217;t Wait for You One of the sharpest points Nico made was about goal-setting. Organizations evolve at linear speed. AI evolves at exponential speed. If you set your targets based on what&#8217;s achievable today, you&#8217;ll spend three years getting there and arrive to find the technology has already moved several times over. The ambition has to account for where the technology is going, not just where it is.

 The Lighthouse Approach Is How You Bring Skeptics Along When I asked Nico how to prepare leadership for a world where teams are managing AI agents instead of doing the work themselves, his answer was the lighthouse. Pick one high-priority area, build a nearly fully agentic version of that team, and let the results speak for themselves. One team owns the underlying platform and data backbone consistently. Separate teams go deep by function or end-to-end process and stay there long enough to actually connect the dots. You don&#8217;t want a team that bounces from marketing to supply chain to finance. You want people who go down the experience curve in a domain and build something real.

 The ROI Is Real, Just Not Magic Nico was refreshingly direct here. A 3 to 4x return on AI technology investment is what BCG is seeing in practice. Not 200x. Not printing money. But a genuine, meaningful return that requires real investment on the tech side, real investment on the people side, and someone senior enough in the organization owning it as their top priority for the next two years minimum.

 The Bottom Line The companies pulling ahead on AI right now aren&#8217;t doing more. They&#8217;re doing fewer things with much higher ambition and much deeper commitment. If you&#8217;re still managing a portfolio of 50 pilots and waiting for one to break through, Nico&#8217;s message was pretty clear: that&#8217;s not a strategy, that&#8217;s a waiting room.
