 Hello GrocerTalk listeners,

 Well, here we are. The very first episode of GrocerTalk.

 I’ll be honest, starting a new podcast is always a slightly strange experience. You spend a lot of time thinking about what you want it to be, and then eventually you have to press record and actually make the thing.

 So, welcome! I hope you enjoy, and please do send me your feedback.

 I’m Ben Miller, your host and editor, and each week I’ll be joined by a different guest host from across the retail industry to help me cut through the noise and focus on the grocery stories that are actually worth paying attention to.

 For our first episode, I couldn’t have asked for a better person to join me than Chris Walton. Chris and I have known each other through the retail world for years, and he brings exactly the kind of frank, straight-talking, occasionally provocative perspective I want GrocerTalk to be known for. He did not disappoint.

 Sponsored by Instacart Enterprise , Portager , and Vusion , this first episode certainly gave us plenty to talk about.

 We covered an $8.7 billion convenience acquisition, Kroger’s e-commerce strategy, AI shopping assistants, voice ordering, and an Amazon automation deal that Chris thinks “should scare the pants off US grocery.”

 So, not exactly easing ourselves in.

 Here’s what caught our attention.

### Couche-Tard Makes a Very Big Bet on Żabka

 We started with Couche-Tard’s planned $8.7 billion acquisition of Żabka, and I was particularly interested in this one because I think it says something much bigger about where convenience is heading.

 Żabka is a very different proposition from the traditional Circle-K model. It’s urban, grocery-led, heavily focused on foodservice, and has built an impressive digital and loyalty ecosystem.

 For me, the big question is what Couche-Tard wants its convenience business to look like in a world where EVs are gradually changing the economics of the gas station, and others like Walmart and Costco use low prices to lock shoppers into their wider ecosystems.

 As I said during the show, they’re essentially buying “something they don’t already have.”

 Chris agreed that the scale of the deal makes this particularly significant, pointing out that it almost doubles Circle-K’s footprint and gives Couche-Tard access to a retailer that is already highly respected within the industry.

 And, in classic Chris fashion, he also reminded me that he’s been told to visit Zabka approximately 700 times but has somehow never managed to get himself to Poland.

 We’ve got to fix that. ( Source )

### Kroger’s E-Commerce Question

 Next up was Kroger, which is now combining grocery and prescription into single home deliveries, powered by Instacart.

 I see that as a sensible move. It makes shopping easier, encourages repeat purchases, and gives Kroger another reason to keep customers within its ecosystem.

 Chris agreed on the prescription piece, seeing it as more “catch-up” than game-changer.

 The bigger question for him was Kroger’s decision to appoint a Chief eCommerce Officer.

 His take was pretty blunt: “I don&#8217;t even think my job should exist.”

 His argument was that the best retailers have moved beyond treating e-commerce as a standalone channel and embraced a truly omnichannel model. Who calls the shots at Kroger now?

 I came at it slightly differently.

 For me, the opportunity is around optimizing fulfillment and the broader digital ecosystem. Kroger needs to work out the right mix of automation and partnerships while building a digital moat that can genuinely differentiate it. And some senior ring-fenching for this might be no bad thing.

 So, yes, we had a few things to debate. Which is probably a good sign for episode one. ( Source )

### Schnucks Wants AI to Become Your Shopping Assistant

 Schnucks Markets has launched a conversational AI tool designed to help shoppers build lists, make recommendations, and navigate nutritional information.

 I think this is one of those stories where the detail can actually distract from what&#8217;s really happening.

 It’s not about tools to build shopping lists, it’s about recognising the way people search is changing.

 We&#8217;ve spent years teaching people to turn questions into keywords. Now tools like Claude and Gemini are teaching people that they can simply ask a question in the way they naturally speak.

 And retailers need to keep up.

 As I said during the show, “You have to keep it within your own digital ecosystem.” The risk of losing product search to the superscalers, with their price comparison capabilities, is too great.

 Chris agreed, but stripped away some of the AI excitement by pointing out that this is, in many ways, simply the next evolution of search.

 And I think that’s exactly right. ( Source )

### Just Eat Wants You to Talk to Your Takeout

 Just Eat is rolling out its AI voice assistant across Europe, with customers able to ask things like, “What’s open that can deliver in under 30 minutes?” or, my personal favourite, “Please just order me my usual curry.” 

 Which, frankly, is probably the dream.

 The company says voice users found what they were looking for 40% faster, but Chris wasn&#8217;t entirely convinced by the numbers.

 His concern was that the comparison depends on how good the original search experience was in the first place.

 I thought there was still something worth watching here, particularly because speed matters enormously in food and grocery.

 Where Chris and I landed was that voice ordering and voice as a new way to search aren&#8217;t necessarily the same thing.

 And I think that distinction is important. ( Source )

### Amazon + AutoStore Is the One to Watch

 Our final headline was the one that really got Chris going.

 Amazon and AutoStore have agreed to a global framework for the supply of automated fulfillment technology. There aren&#8217;t any purchasing commitments just yet, but as Chris put it:

 “There’s rarely any smoke without fire.”

 I think the interesting question is what Amazon might ultimately be trying to solve for in grocery.

 Its existing fulfillment operation is incredibly sophisticated, but grocery brings different challenges, particularly around multiple temperature zones, micro-fulfillment, and the increasingly important race to deliver quickly.

 Chris took the thought a little further than I did.

 His warning?

 “This announcement should scare the pants off US grocery.”

 His argument is that Amazon already has the customer traffic. If it can pair that with an increasingly sophisticated grocery fulfillment operation, the combination could be pretty formidable.

 And when you put this alongside Walmart&#8217;s continued investment in automation, I think there is a much bigger story emerging.

 Too many people have looked at what’s happened with the Kroger and Ocado partnership and decided the future of online grocery fulfilment shouldn’t be automated. For me, that’s a huge misread.

 Optimizing store pick, choosing the right partners, and automating when scale and density allows is key. ( Source )

### Then We Got to Grab & Go

 And because five big headlines apparently weren&#8217;t enough for our first episode, we finished with GrocerTalk Grab & Go.

 Chris brought Walmart&#8217;s latest automated micro-fulfillment investment to the table, continuing the discussion from the AutoStore story above, which led me to a fairly straightforward conclusion:

 “The future of US grocery is going to be more automated as opposed to less automated.”

 I brought a story from the UK about Waitrose&#8217;s new AI assistant for store associates, Rosie 2.0.

 It can help employees with everything from product availability and nutritional information to wine pairings.

 Yes, wine pairings.

 Because if you&#8217;re going to give an AI assistant a job in Waitrose, it seems only right that it should know its way around a Burgundy.

 Chris, naturally, wanted to know whether I&#8217;d be happy asking an AI for my wine recommendation while standing in the wine aisle.

 My answer was essentially: if it gives me a better recommendation, I&#8217;m all in.

 Chris&#8217;s response?

 “If my beef tastes better with my wine. 100%.”

 And with that, we probably had the appropriate ending for episode one.

### Welcome to GrocerTalk
