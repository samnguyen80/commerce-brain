 Hello Omni Talk Fans!

 After two weeks off, I’m back and there was a lot to catch up on this week, including the launch of our brand-new GrocerTalk podcast , another great OmniStar Award winner, and, of course, a new round of retail headlines to debate.

 This week, I was joined by Jenn Hahn of J Recruiting Services, a retail talent expert and longtime Omni Talk guest. Jenn and I agreed on plenty this week, but we also had a few moments where we saw things very differently, particularly when it came to Kroger’s decision to create a new chief e-commerce officer role.

 And that’s exactly what makes these conversations fun.

 Here’s what we covered in this week’s OmniTalk Retail Fast Five, sponsored by the A&M Consumer and Retail Group , Mirakl , Ocampo Capital , Quorso , Veloq , and R&S Logistics :

### Ralph Woodward Named This Month’s OmniStar

 This week also marked the latest edition of our OmniStar Award, presented in partnership with Quorso .

 The award recognizes the real operators making a difference inside retail organizations, not the pundits or self-proclaimed experts.

 Jenn selected Ralph Woodward, Senior Vice President of Retail Operations at Bashas’, Food City, AJ’s, and Tribal Stores.

 And it’s hard to think of someone more deserving.

 Ralph has spent roughly four decades in retail and has seen virtually every version of the industry. Jenn recently had him on her own podcast, where he shared stories about his career, the leadership lessons he learned along the way, and the sheer resilience required to stay in retail for that long.

 “He is that true legacy ops leader,” Jenn said.

 I also have a personal connection to the selection. Bashas’ was my hometown grocery store growing up, so seeing someone who has spent so much of his career helping lead the business receive this recognition was particularly meaningful.

 Congratulations, Ralph.

### Kroger’s E-Commerce Bet Raises A Bigger Organizational Question

 Our conversation about Kroger ultimately became bigger than just one executive appointment.

 Kroger named Nate Faust, a former Jet.com and Walmart executive, as its first-ever Chief E-Commerce Officer, creating a new leadership role at a time when much of retail has been moving toward eliminating the distinction between e-commerce and stores altogether.

 Jenn pointed out that the move could signal Kroger simply wants someone waking up every day focused exclusively on fixing and growing its e-commerce business.

 Her argument was especially interesting given Kroger’s recent progress toward e-commerce profitability.

 For Jenn, the appointment may actually be a sign that Kroger knows there is still a lot of work to do.

 “They’ve sort of, everyone else is moving towards omnichannel, but it’s almost like we’re not ready to move towards omnichannel until we have enough figured out in this e-commerce,” she said.

 I took the opposite view.

 To me, the organizational structure is strange, particularly because Kroger already has a chief digital officer. I also questioned whether Faust’s background gives him the operational experience necessary to oversee such a complex business, and whether creating a standalone e-commerce function could ultimately create friction with merchandising.

 At the heart of our disagreement was a much bigger question: Is e-commerce really where Kroger needs to differentiate, or is it simply something the company needs to get better at to keep pace with the competition? ( Source )

### Best Buy Thinks Smaller Is The Path To Growth

 Best Buy’s incoming CEO says the path to growth starts with going smaller, as the retailer looks to rethink its store footprint.

 Jenn and I debated whether shrinking the physical footprint can actually create meaningful growth or whether it risks limiting the retailer’s ability to showcase the breadth of its assortment.

 The bigger question is what consumers actually need from a Best Buy store today.

 As more purchases move online, the store increasingly has to justify the trip through experience, services, expertise, and the products customers actually want to see and touch.

 For Best Buy, going smaller could ultimately make the stores more productive, but only if the company is ruthless about deciding what deserves the space. ( Source )

### Ahold Brings Savings Kiosks To Giant Food

 Ahold Delhaize is expanding its Saving Station discount kiosks to Giant Food, giving shoppers another way to access personalized savings inside the physical store.

 The move raises an interesting question about the role physical technology still plays in an increasingly digital loyalty environment.

 As loyalty programs, personalized offers, and digital coupons continue moving into shoppers’ phones, kiosks can seem like an old-school solution.

 But that may actually be their advantage.

 Not every grocery shopper wants to manage every promotion through an app, and putting savings directly into the store experience could help bridge the gap between traditional grocery shopping and increasingly sophisticated digital personalization.

 The challenge will be making the experience genuinely useful rather than simply adding another piece of technology to the store. ( Source )

### Amazon Shoppers Are Turning To Alexa

 Amazon customers are increasingly using Alexa for shopping, another sign that voice and AI could become a much bigger part of how consumers discover and purchase products.

 The interesting part is that Amazon may finally be moving beyond using AI simply to recommend products.

 The bigger opportunity is having an AI agent actually complete the transaction.

 That shift from “What should I buy?” to “Buy this for me” could fundamentally change the role search, product pages, and even Amazon’s own shopping interface play in the customer journey.

 If Amazon can make that experience seamless, Alexa could become much more than a voice assistant. It could become a true shopping agent. ( Source )

### TikTok Shop Looks To Build Its Own Prime

 TikTok is reportedly testing a paid Shop Plus membership, potentially giving the platform its own version of Amazon Prime.

 That would be a fascinating next step for TikTok Shop.

 TikTok has already proven it can drive discovery and impulse purchases. The next challenge is keeping shoppers inside the ecosystem once they decide they want something.

 A membership program could help TikTok move from being a place where consumers discover products to a place where they regularly shop for them.

 If TikTok can combine its powerful discovery engine with the retention mechanics of a membership program, Amazon may have a much more interesting competitor on its hands than simply another social commerce platform. ( Source )

### Five Insightful Minutes With Arber Sejdiji Of Zenline AI

 We also sat down for five insightful minutes with Arber Sejdiji of Zenline AI to talk about one of the biggest questions facing retail organizations right now: how merchandising teams should actually think about deploying agentic AI.

 Rather than treating AI as another technology project, Arber shared his perspective on how retailers can think about AI as a tool for helping merchandising teams work differently and make better decisions.

 It was a timely conversation as retailers move from experimenting with generative AI toward figuring out where autonomous AI agents can actually create value inside the business.

 Of course, it also wouldn’t be a Fast Five without a little fun.
