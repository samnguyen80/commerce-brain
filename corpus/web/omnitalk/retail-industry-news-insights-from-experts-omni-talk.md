 Hello Omni Talk Fans! 

 One of my favorite parts of Fast Five is welcoming guests who have actually lived the challenges behind the headlines.

 This week, I was joined by Josh Hahn , Director of Growth at J Recruiting Services and former retail executive at Meijer and Sports Authority, who joked early in the show that retail wasn&#8217;t exactly the career he had planned. He thought he&#8217;d become an engineer. Instead, after landing a job stocking shelves at Target as a teenager, he caught what he called the &#8220;retail bug&#8221; and never looked back.

 I&#8217;m certainly glad he did. Whether we were debating employee influencers, grocery economics, or organizational restructuring, Josh consistently brought the discussion back to what actually works inside a retail business.

 Here&#8217;s what we covered in this week&#8217;s OmniTalk Retail Fast Five, sponsored by the A&M Consumer and Retail Group ,  Mirakl ,  Ocampo Capital ,  Quorso ,  Veloq , and  R&S Logistics: 

### Gap Wants Employees To Become Influencers

 Gap Inc. is expanding its creator program by allowing employees across its stores, offices, and distribution centers to become paid brand influencers.

 Josh immediately loved the idea, pointing out that today&#8217;s consumers trust people far more than traditional marketing. In his view, retailers already have thousands of authentic advocates working inside their businesses every day, so why not empower them to tell the brand story?

 His only caution? Don&#8217;t over-engineer it.

 The second retailers bury the program beneath approval workflows and corporate guardrails, they risk losing the authenticity that makes employee-generated content so valuable in the first place.

 I couldn&#8217;t agree more. In fact, this is something I&#8217;ve been talking about for years. Some of the smartest product experts in retail are merchants, buyers, and store associates. They&#8217;re the people who know exactly why a product was created, what makes it different, and who it&#8217;s for. Giving those people a platform feels like one of the smartest marketing moves we&#8217;ve seen in a long time. ( Source )

### David&#8217;s Bridal Blends Full Price And Outlet Shopping

 David&#8217;s Bridal is testing a new outlet shop-in-shop concept that combines discounted inventory alongside its traditional bridal assortment.

 At first glance, neither Josh nor I loved the idea.

 But the more we talked through it, the more we realized bridal shopping simply isn&#8217;t like traditional apparel shopping.

 Josh emphasized that execution will determine whether this succeeds. The outlet experience has to feel intentional and separate enough that it doesn&#8217;t diminish the premium experience brides expect.

 What ultimately changed my mind was thinking about how unique wedding shopping really is. Customers have a limited amount of time to find the right dress, often with a fixed budget. Giving them premium and outlet options in one visit may actually make the experience easier rather than more confusing.

 Sometimes retail rules have exceptions. This just might be one of them. ( Source )

### Grocery&#8217;s Challenges Run Deeper Than Inflation

 New data showed grocery unit sales declined for the fifth consecutive month.

 Josh doesn&#8217;t believe this is a temporary slowdown.

 His argument was simple: inflation may have started the problem, but consumers are fundamentally changing how they shop. Warehouse clubs continue gaining share, shoppers remain focused on everyday value, and retailers may ultimately need actual deflation&#8230; not simply slower inflation&#8230; to reverse the trend.

 I agreed but also pointed to several other forces reshaping grocery at the same time, from changing SNAP regulations to the continued growth of online grocery and delivery.

 Taken together, they paint a picture of an industry navigating structural change rather than simply waiting for consumer demand to bounce back. ( Source )

### Hy-Vee Bets On Speed

 Hy-Vee announced the rollout of 30-minute pickup and delivery across all of its stores.

 Josh viewed the move less as a profit driver and more as a loyalty strategy.

 Fast fulfillment is expensive, but if it encourages more shoppers to join Perks Plus and remain loyal to the brand, the long-term value extends far beyond any individual delivery order.

 I saw it much the same way.

 Thirty-minute fulfillment is quickly becoming table stakes as Walmart, Amazon, Instacart, and others continue raising customer expectations. The challenge is no longer simply offering speed—it&#8217;s executing it consistently better than the competition. ( Source )

### Albertsons Reorganizes For The Future

 Albertsons unveiled its ACI Edge restructuring plan, consolidating divisions while centralizing merchandising and other key functions.

 Josh actually found the announcement surprising.

 Having spent years inside merchandising organizations, he expected far more centralization already existed. While there are always risks in moving decision-making farther from the local market, he believes consolidating buying power and creating greater operational consistency makes a great deal of sense.

 Our conversation eventually shifted toward an even bigger question.

 As pricing becomes increasingly transparent and digital competition intensifies, can regional grocery banners continue competing against Walmart, Amazon, and Aldi over the long term?

 That&#8217;s a much bigger challenge than any organizational restructuring alone can solve. ( Source )
