 Hello Omni Talk Fans! I am just back from three weeks in Europe and was excited to find this great omnichannel retailing headline out of Lowe’s to kick off the week.

 In today’s Retail Daily Minute: 

 

- Lowe&#8217;s integrates its third-party marketplace into its My Red Vest associate software , putting thousands of marketplace products directly in the hands of store employees at over 1,700 locations.

- Coca-Cola, PepsiCo, and Keurig Dr Pepper roll out QR codes  linking consumers to the American Beverage Association&#8217;s Good to Know database.

- REI gets roasted online after Meta&#8217;s AI ad tool auto-generated an image of a physically impossible two-handlebar bike , prompting the retailer to unenroll from the tool and issue an apology.
 

 Plus, my 5 Insightful Minutes conversation with Rohlik Group’s Rita Kerbaj coming off of my tour of their Vienna grocery fulfillment center last week, and a quote from a film that I was told shockingly few Austrians have ever seen.

 Today’s Top Headlines 

 1. Lowe&#8217;s Brings Its Marketplace Directly Into Stores via New Tool 

 Lowe&#8217;s integrated its third-party marketplace into My Red Vest, letting store associates search and order marketplace products for customers with direct home delivery. Online sales rose 15.5% in Q1, with marketplace as a key driver. The assortment has more than doubled over the past year. ( Read More )

 2. Beverage Giants Add QR Codes to Boost Ingredient Transparency 

 Coca-Cola, PepsiCo and Keurig Dr Pepper are adding QR codes to packaging linking to the American Beverage Association&#8217;s Good to Know database, covering 140 ingredients with safety data from the FDA, European Food Safety Authority and Health Canada. Full industry rollout is committed by the end of 2027. ( Read More )

 3. REI Blames Meta AI Tool for Botched Bike Image in Instagram Ad 

 REI faced social media backlash after a Meta AI tool auto-enrolled the retailer and altered a vendor bike image in Instagram ads, producing a two-handlebar bicycle. REI called the alteration &#8220;inaccurate and deeply inappropriate,&#8221; saying Meta&#8217;s AI personalization feature was applied without their consent. ( Read More )

 4. GameStop&#8217;s Cohen Drops $35B Pay to Stay Focused on eBay Deal 

 GameStop CEO Ryan Cohen withdrew his $35 billion pay package to focus fully on acquiring eBay, which rejected his cash-and-stock offer in May. Cohen plans to commit $500 million of his own money and envisions using GameStop&#8217;s 1,600 stores for live commerce fulfillment and content creation studios. ( Read More )

 5. Starbucks Pilots a Custom TikTok Creator Network With Its Staff 

 Starbucks became the first brand to pilot a custom Creator Network with TikTok, expanding its employee-driven Green Apron Creators program to include ad revenue sharing with select staff creators. Starbucks employees post at three times the rate of peers at similar-sized chains, fueling the paid content strategy. ( Read More )
