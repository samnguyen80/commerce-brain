 Hello Omni Talk Fans! 

 One of my favorite parts of The Fast Five each month is welcoming members of the A&M Consumer and Retail Group back to the show.

 Their experience leading merchandising, pricing, analytics, and retail strategy always takes the conversation beyond the headlines, and this month&#8217;s discussion with Senior Directors Brandon Pezely and Alex Brakebill was no exception. From debating whether DoorDash is quietly becoming the &#8220;marketplace of marketplaces&#8221; to questioning if Wonder&#8217;s $9 billion valuation is built on substance or hype, we spent a lot of time separating great ideas from great businesses.

 Here&#8217;s what we covered in this week&#8217;s OmniTalk Retail Fast Five, sponsored by the A&M Consumer and Retail Group , Mirakl , Ocampo Capital , Quorso , Veloq , and R&S Logistics :

### DoorDash Is Thinking Bigger Than Food Delivery

 DoorDash announced a native integration with Shopify that lets merchants publish their catalogs directly to the DoorDash marketplace without additional onboarding or manual work.

 Alex saw the announcement as a meaningful improvement for independent retailers, particularly those looking to simplify inventory management and reach new customers. His hesitation centered around one familiar concern: as retailers become more dependent on third-party marketplaces, they also risk giving away ownership of the customer relationship.

 Brandon agreed but looked at the announcement through a different lens. Scale matters. Shopify has already proven the value of shared technology infrastructure, and DoorDash is doing something similar with delivery. By bringing more merchants onto the platform, DoorDash improves the economics for everyone involved.

 I was probably the most bullish of the group. DoorDash continues making smart strategic moves, from DashPass to AI-powered search, and this partnership feels like another step toward becoming what I called the &#8220;marketplace of marketplaces.&#8221; The execution still matters, especially around fulfillment, but it&#8217;s hard not to be impressed by the direction they&#8217;re heading. ( Source )

### Is OpenAI Building The Next Great Device?

 OpenAI is reportedly developing a screenless AI companion device alongside legendary Apple designer Jony Ive.

 None of us were rushing to preorder one.

 Brandon admitted he&#8217;s always loved the promise of voice technology but feels the experience has consistently fallen short. More importantly, he questioned what happens as people rely more heavily on AI companions instead of real human relationships. It&#8217;s a fascinating question that extends well beyond technology.

 Alex was even more skeptical, pointing out that most consumers already carry an iPhone capable of handling many of these tasks. Rather than building another piece of hardware, he argued OpenAI should stay focused on making its AI models better.

 I found myself somewhere in the middle. Part of me wonders whether we&#8217;re making the same mistake people made when they dismissed the first iPhone. At the same time, this product feels rushed, leaving plenty of questions about whether it&#8217;s the next computing platform or simply another interesting experiment. ( Source )

### Good Intentions Don&#8217;t Always Guarantee Good Outcomes

 The European Union officially banned large companies from destroying unsold clothing, footwear, and accessories in an effort to reduce waste and encourage resale, donation, and recycling.

 Everyone agreed the motivation behind the regulation is admirable.

 Where the conversation became more interesting was around execution. Alex questioned how regulators will realistically monitor inventory across massive global supply chains and warned that companies may simply move excess inventory elsewhere. Brandon agreed, noting that executives are ultimately responsible for maximizing shareholder value, making workarounds an inevitable part of the equation.

 For me, the biggest takeaway wasn&#8217;t necessarily this specific regulation. It was the broader trend. Sustainability legislation isn&#8217;t slowing down, and retailers should expect similar conversations to continue spreading beyond Europe in the years ahead. ( Source )

### Kroger Is Right To Keep Experimenting

 Kroger is testing premium in-store wine shops featuring curated assortments, tasting bars, and trained wine stewards.

 Brandon immediately liked the concept, not because wine alone will transform Kroger&#8217;s business, but because retailers should constantly be testing new ideas. As grocery competition continues splitting between value players and specialty retailers, finding meaningful ways to differentiate has become increasingly important.

 Alex pointed to examples like H-E-B and Kroger&#8217;s own bar concepts, arguing that localized experiences absolutely can work when deployed in the right markets. The key is recognizing that not every store needs the same solution.

 I agreed the test is worth running. Whether wine shops become a long-term growth driver remains to be seen, but retailers rarely discover new opportunities by standing still. Testing, learning, and refining is exactly what companies like Kroger should be doing. ( Source )

### Wonder Still Has A Lot To Prove

 Marc Lore says Wonder is preparing for an IPO after reaching a reported $9 billion valuation.

 Alex acknowledged the operational logic behind centralized kitchens and shared infrastructure but questioned whether consumers are actually excited by generic food hall concepts. Brandon shared many of the same concerns, particularly around the company&#8217;s long path to profitability and whether the underlying technology creates enough competitive advantage.

 The number I kept coming back to was the valuation itself.

 Nine billion dollars is an enormous number for a food business. While Wonder continues attracting investors and expanding rapidly, it&#8217;s still difficult to understand exactly what makes the model deserving of that premium. At some point, the business fundamentals have to match the story.

 Alex also raised one of the most interesting points of the episode. As GLP-1 medications continue changing eating habits, the long-term demand assumptions behind food delivery businesses may look very different than they did just a few years ago. That&#8217;s a trend every retailer should be watching closely. ( Source )
