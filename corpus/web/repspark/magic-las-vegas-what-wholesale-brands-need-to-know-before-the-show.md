# MAGIC Las Vegas: What Wholesale Brands Need to Know Before the Show 

 
 
 
 by Sawyer Frank on August 7, 2026
 

 
 MAGIC Las Vegas is the largest fashion trade show in the United States, three days on the floor of the Las Vegas Convention Center where the decisions that drive a wholesale brand's fall season get made, unmade, or missed entirely depending on how prepared you were before you arrived.

 
 For wholesale brands attending MAGIC, the show floor looks different than it did five years ago. Buyers are moving faster. Appointments are shorter. The brands closing business on the floor are the ones with digital infrastructure in place before they unpack their booth. The brands leaving with business cards and follow-up intentions are the ones that didn't.

 Here is what you need to know — and do — before MAGIC opens.

## Why MAGIC Is a Different Kind of Show

 MAGIC has always been where fashion wholesale happens at scale. Apparel, activewear, contemporary, menswear, womenswear, accessories — the full range of the industry compressed into a few days in Las Vegas, with thousands of retail buyers walking the floor looking for what's next.

 What's changed in 2026 is the expectation buyers bring with them. A buyer who manages wholesale accounts for a regional chain is running a business shaped by digital consumer experiences everywhere else in their life. They expect live inventory. They expect to browse and order digitally. They expect the brand they're meeting with to have their information ready before the appointment starts — not assembled from memory while standing in the booth.

 The brands that meet that expectation win the appointment. The brands that don't end up on the follow-up list — which is where orders go to die.

## What to Have Ready Before the Show Opens

### Your Digital Catalog Has to Be Live and Accurate

 Every style you're presenting at MAGIC 2026 needs to be current in your ordering platform before your first appointment. That means confirmed inventory, current pricing, accurate delivery timelines, and any show-specific terms applied to the right product groups.

 RepSpark syncs directly with your ERP so the catalog buyers see on the show floor reflects what's actually available to ship — not a PDF you built last month. Before you leave for Las Vegas, pull a live inventory report, reconcile it against what you're presenting, and update anything that's changed. A buyer who orders a style on the floor and receives a cancellation the following week is not a buyer you'll see at your booth next year.

### Build Event Microsites Before You Leave the Office

 MAGIC runs three days. Your most productive selling window may be shorter than that — blocked appointments, competing meetings, and a show floor that exhausts everyone by day two. Event microsites extend your reach beyond those windows.

 Before the show, build a RepSpark microsite for your MAGIC lineup: your show collection, any floor exclusives, and your show specials. Share the link in your pre-show outreach, display it at the booth, and include it in every post-show follow-up. Buyers who run out of time during the show can complete their order from the microsite link on their flight home. Buyers who weren't ready to commit on the floor get a direct path back to your collection without a rep having to manually re-present it.

 The microsite does the follow-up work your reps don't have time to do.

### Create Account-Specific Presentations for Your Priority Meetings

 You know your appointment list before you land in Las Vegas. For your top 15 to 20 accounts — the buyers you've confirmed time with — build a custom assortment in RepSpark before you arrive.

 A contemporary boutique doesn't need to see your full activewear line. A multi-door chain buyer for a sporting goods retailer doesn't need the resort collection. Custom assortments communicate that you understand your buyer's business before they have to explain it — which accelerates every conversation toward an order and away from a product overview.

 Brands that walk into MAGIC appointments with tailored presentations close faster. Brands that open the full catalog and scroll until something lands leave more to chance.

### Train Your Team on the Platform Before You Hit the Floor

 MAGIC appointments are 30 to 45 minutes on a good day. On a busy show floor, they're shorter. Every minute your rep spends fumbling with a platform they haven't practiced is a minute they're not building the order — and the buyer who has three more meetings before lunch will move on.

 Run a team training session at least one week before the show. Every rep on your team should be able to open RepSparkGo on a tablet, pull up a buyer's account, build a customized cart against live inventory, apply show pricing, and submit a completed order in under 10 minutes without help. Practice it in the office. Practice it on real accounts. The platform should be invisible by the time it's being used in front of a buyer.

 This is not a nice-to-have. At a show like MAGIC, where the density of buyer appointments is higher than almost anywhere else in the industry calendar, rep fluency with the ordering platform is the difference between a meeting that closes and a meeting that follows up.

### Lock Your Show Pricing and Terms Into the Platform Before You Leave

 If you're running MAGIC specials — extended payment terms, a free freight threshold, a promotional incentive on pre-book orders — those terms need to be in the platform before your first appointment, not negotiated rep-by-rep on the floor.

 When show pricing is built into RepSpark, every rep applies it consistently, every buyer gets the same offer, and nobody is improvising terms that create problems when the order processes. It also frees your reps to sell rather than explain — a conversation about what the offer is costs time that should go toward building the order.

## On the Floor

### Scan Every Badge. Every One.

 MAGIC draws tens of thousands of buyers across its show days. The buyers who stop at your booth, linger over a particular style, ask one question and keep moving — those are leads. Every one of them who leaves without a badge scan is a contact you don't have, a follow-up you can't send, and an order you'll never know you could have closed.

 Make badge scanning mandatory from the first hour of day one. Every rep, every conversation. No exceptions for "I'll remember them" or "they gave me their card." Badge scan data is what your post-show follow-up is built on — and at a show the size of MAGIC, the difference between 40 quality follow-ups and 120 is often just whether someone scanned.

### Close Orders on the Floor — Not in the Hotel Room

 The order submitted during the appointment is the order that ships cleanly. The order reconstructed at midnight from handwritten notes and an annotated catalog page is the order with the wrong style number, the wrong quantity, and the colorway the buyer didn't actually want.

 RepSparkGo is built for the show floor. Open it, build the order against live inventory, confirm it with the buyer, submit it before they stand up. They get a confirmation. You get a committed order. Your rep moves to the next meeting without a stack of notes to process later.

 At MAGIC 2026, the brands writing and closing orders in the room will outperform the brands writing orders after the show closes — every time.

### Debrief Daily. Adjust Mid-Show.

 MAGIC is three days. What's resonating on day one informs what you lead with on day two. The style that's generating the most conversation might not be the style you built your presentation around. The show special that isn't landing might need to be repositioned or deprioritized in favor of what buyers are actually responding to.

 Every morning before the floor opens, 10 minutes with your team: What's working? What isn't? Which accounts showed up and which didn't? Who needs a follow-up today rather than after the show? The brands that adapt mid-show close more business over the full three days than the brands that run the same pitch every appointment regardless of what the floor is telling them.

## After the Show

### 48 Hours. That's Your Window.

 Buyers who leave MAGIC on Wednesday return to their stores on Thursday. By Friday they've processed other orders, responded to other brands, and started on the next thing. The brands that follow up on Thursday and Friday are talking to buyers who still remember the conversation. The brands that follow up the following week are talking to buyers who have moved on.

 Within 48 hours of the show closing, every badge scan should receive an outreach. Segment it: buyers who placed orders get a confirmation and a thank-you with order details. Buyers who were interested but didn't order get a follow-up with your microsite link, a reminder of the show special, and a clear deadline. Cold contacts get a lighter touch — a catalog link and a single call to action.

 This is the part of the trade show playbook most brands underinvest in. The close rate on 48-hour follow-up after MAGIC is meaningfully higher than the close rate on follow-up that happens a week later. The window is short. Use it.
