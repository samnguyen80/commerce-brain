# Wholesale Fashion Trends Recap, What Sold at Market This August 

 
 
 
 by Tim McLain on August 18, 2026
 

 
 Buyers wrote fall and holiday orders this month against a consumer picture that is better than the headlines suggest.

 The Census Bureau released July retail figures on August 14. Total retail and food services sales fell 0.6% from June. Clothing and clothing accessories stores went the other way, rising 1.9% month over month to about 28.3 billion dollars on a seasonally adjusted basis, and running roughly 5% ahead of July last year.

 That divergence matters more than either number alone. In a month when overall retail spending contracted, apparel and accessories expanded. Buyers arriving in Atlanta the following week were not shopping defensively against a soft category. They were shopping a category that had just outperformed the rest of retail.

 It is worth being precise about the limits here. One month is not a trend, advance estimates get revised, and store level sales tell you nothing about margin. But it is the most current hard data available, and it is more useful than sentiment.

## What Buyers Walked In Shopping For

 Atlanta Apparel published its Autumn/Winter 2026 and 2027 trend report in March, and it framed the season as one defined by romantic nostalgia and modern bohemia, pulling references from the 60s, 80s, and 90s through retro mod silhouettes and vintage inspired glamour with darker whimsical undertones.

 "Autumn/Winter 2026 highlights a renewed appetite for romantic nostalgia, reimagined for today's consumer. Soft volume, heritage influences, and layered styling create a mood that feels both expressive and wearable," said Caron Stover, Senior Vice President Apparel at ANDMORE.

 The specifics are worth writing down, because this is the vocabulary your buyers were using on the floor.

 In silhouettes, pleated balloon trousers, fluted miniskirts, romantic blouses with fluid sleeves, coordinated cardigan and scarf sets, funnel neck bombers, mini pinafore dresses, and layered knits.

 In color, Bright Emerald, Cranberry Juice, Steel Blue, Cocoa Powder, Wax Paper, and denim blues.

 In footwear, slouchy boots, pointed ballet flats, Mary Janes, kitten heels, slingbacks, and biker boots.

 In materials and pattern, heritage checks, dark opulent prints, fluffy textures, soft suede, and polished metal hardware.

 In accessories, slouchy top handle bags, belt layering, bonnets, triangle scarves, brooches, and long pendant necklaces.

 Read that list as a demand signal rather than a style forecast. Soft volume, layered styling, and heritage reference all point the same commercial direction, which is toward pieces that work across more of the calendar. Buyers hedging against weather volatility and a compressed holiday buy want product that does not expire in six weeks.

## Where Atlanta Concentrated This August

 Ahead of the August market, ANDMORE emphasized expansion in occasion and formal categories, specifically bridal and quinceañera programming, alongside contemporary and occasion wear, children's apparel, footwear, and fashion accessories. The Formal Markets ran a day longer than Apparel, through August 7.

 The showroom activity tells you where the organizers see growth. Formal debuts included De Blossom Collection, Delicious Shoes, MNM Couture, and Pure Silk Fabrics, with expansions from ASHLEYlauren introducing its SCALA line, Rachel Allan, Nox Anabel, Badgley Mischka, and Nella Pima.

 Buyer programming leaned hard into occasion as well, with a Wedding Masterclass, a Bridal Breakfast education series, and a Bridal Passport collection program.

 The pattern is consistent. Occasion wear is where the market is placing its bets, and occasion is a category with a structural advantage right now, because a wedding, a quinceañera, or a homecoming is a purchase with a fixed date that does not get deferred when consumers get cautious.

## The Number That Matters More Than the Trend Report

 The most useful data point of the 2026 season so far did not come from August at all.

 When Atlanta Market and Atlanta Apparel ran together in June, from the 9th through the 14th, total buying accounts rose 1.1% year over year. Apparel category buying accounts rose 53%. First time participants were up 22.5%.

 A 53% increase in apparel buying accounts against a 1.1% increase in total accounts is not a rising tide. It is apparel specifically pulling new buyers into the building.

 Amory Wooden, Chief Revenue Officer at ANDMORE, attributed it to retailers wanting "greater efficiency, broader discovery, and opportunities to source across categories in one destination." Exhibitors reported the same thing from the floor. Hunter Freese of Dolce Vita said the brand "saw so many new accounts that we've never worked with before."

 For any brand reading this, that is the actionable finding. The buyers in the aisles this season include an unusually high proportion of accounts you have never sold. New account acquisition at market is running well ahead of normal, and the follow up on those conversations is worth more than it was two years ago.

## The Only Honest Answer to What Sold

 Here is the uncomfortable truth about market recaps, including this one.

 Trend reports tell you what was shown. Attendance figures tell you who came. Neither tells you what wrote. The organizers do not have that data, the trade press cannot get it, and by the time any aggregate analysis appears, you will have already made your reorder and production decisions.

 The only real time read on what sold at market is your own order data, and most brands cannot see it clearly enough to act on it in August.

 The questions worth answering this week are specific. Which styles moved from assortment views into carts, and which got looked at and skipped. What your at once mix looked like relative to prebook, since at once demand in August is the cleanest signal of retailer confidence. Which accounts wrote for the first time and what they chose, because a first order tells you how a new buyer reads your line without a rep steering them. Which carts were built at market and never submitted, since those are live and recoverable this week rather than next season.

 That is the analysis that changes a decision. It requires order data that is structured, current, and visible to the people who need it, rather than sitting in an ERP that the sales and merchandising teams cannot query.

## Dallas Is Next

 The Dallas Apparel and Accessories Market runs August 18 through 21, alongside KidsWorld, AETA, and the WESA western and English market. If you are exhibiting, the one thing worth changing before Tuesday is how fast a written order becomes a confirmed order.

 The buyers walking those aisles have now been to Atlanta or Las Vegas, or both. They have seen the season. They have a narrowing open to buy and a lengthening list of brands asking for it. The brand that confirms an order with a real ship date before the buyer leaves the building has an advantage over the brand that promises to follow up next week.
