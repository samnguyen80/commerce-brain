# A Pre-Market Checklist: 10 Things to Prep Before Your Next PGA Trade Show 

 
 
 
 by Tim McLain on August 7, 2026
 

 
 The brands that win at trade shows don't wing it.

 Whether you're heading to the PGA Buying Summit in July or setting up a booth at the PGA Show in Orlando in January, the difference between a show that generates six figures in orders and one that generates a stack of business cards you'll never follow up on is almost entirely decided before you leave the office.

 This checklist covers what to do before, during, and after organized around the mistakes that cost brands the most, and the tools that eliminate them.

## Before the Show

### 1. Audit Your Digital Catalog and Confirm Live Inventory

 If you're showing product at a trade show, your buyers expect to order it. Nothing damages a rep's credibility faster than a buyer who wants to place an order on a style shown in the booth only to find out it's out of stock, discontinued, or only available in one colorway.

 Before you finalize your show presentation, pull a live inventory report. Remove or flag styles that won't be available in time to fulfill post-show orders. Update your digital catalog to reflect current availability, pricing, and delivery timelines. If you're using RepSpark, confirm that your ERP sync is current so that buyers on the show floor see accurate stock in real time — not a snapshot from last month.

 This takes a few hours before the show. It saves hours of cancellation conversations after it.

### 2. Build Event Microsites for Show Specials and Product Launches

 Trade shows are one of the highest-leverage moments to capture advance orders on new product — if you give buyers a way to order before they leave your booth.

 RepSpark's event microsites let you build a branded digital storefront for a specific product launch, a show exclusive, or a promotional offer available only during the show window. You share the link in your booth, in pre-show outreach, and in follow-up emails. Buyers who don't have time to place a full order during the show can complete it on their own time after, without your rep having to chase them down.

 Set up your microsite before you leave. Test the link. Make sure the products, pricing, and terms are exactly what your reps will be presenting on the floor.

### 3. Build Account-Specific Assortments for Your Priority Buyers

 You know who your most important meetings are with before you arrive. Build for them.

 For your top 10 to 20 accounts — the buyers you have confirmed appointments with — create a custom assortment in RepSpark that reflects what's relevant to their channel, geography, and buy history. A specialty golf account at a private club doesn't need to see your full swimwear line. A resort retailer buying for three locations needs to see styles that work across different environments.

 Walking into a pre-scheduled meeting with a tailored presentation rather than a generic catalog communicates that you've done the work before the appointment. It also shortens the time it takes to get to an order, which matters when every appointment is 30 to 45 minutes.

### 4. Set Show Pricing, Specials, and Terms in the Platform Before You Leave

 Show specials work when every rep applies them consistently. They don't work when one rep forgets the terms, another quotes the wrong discount tier, and the buyer who got a better deal tells the buyer who didn't.

 Before the show, set your show-specific pricing, promotional terms, and any order incentives directly in RepSpark. When the offer is built into the platform, it's consistent across every rep, every conversation, and every order — regardless of who writes it or when.

 This also means your reps are selling the show special rather than explaining it. Those are different conversations.

### 5. Train Your Entire Sales Team on the Ordering Platform

 This is the item most brands underinvest in, and the one with the highest cost when it's skipped.

 Every rep who will be on the show floor needs to be able to open RepSparkGo on a tablet or phone, pull up a buyer's account, build an assortment, and submit a completed order in under 10 minutes — without help. If your reps are fumbling with the platform during an appointment, you've lost the window. Buyers at trade shows are moving fast. The rep who can close the order in the room gets the order. The rep who says "I'll follow up with the order form" often doesn't.

 Run a team training session at least a week before the show — not the night before. Give reps time to practice on real accounts so the tool is second nature by the time they're standing in front of a buyer.

### 6. Build Your Appointment Calendar and Send Pre-Show Outreach

 The best trade show meetings are scheduled before the show opens. Buyers with confirmed appointments show up prepared to buy. Buyers without appointments are harder to convert in the aisle.

 Three to four weeks before the PGA Buying Summit or PGA Show, send personalized outreach to your top accounts: a preview of what you're showing, a note about your show specials, and a direct link to schedule time. Include your event microsite link so interested buyers can start browsing before they arrive.

 A confirmed appointment calendar also tells you how to staff the booth. If you have 30 scheduled meetings and four reps, you know exactly how much coverage you need and when.

## On the Show Floor

### 7. Scan Every Badge — Every Single One

 This is the highest-ROI item on this list, and the one most brands treat as an afterthought.

 Badge scanners capture contact information from every person who walks into your booth or stops to look at your product. A conversation that doesn't end in an order is not a lost conversation — it's a lead that needs follow-up. A buyer who says "I'm not ready to place an order today" and hands you a business card that you lose on the way back to the hotel is a buyer you won't reach again until the next show.

 Make badge scanning mandatory for every rep, every conversation, from the first hour of day one. At the PGA Buying Summit and PGA Show, badge scan data integrates directly with your CRM follow-up workflow. The rep who scans 80 badges and follows up with all of them will outperform the rep who closes 15 orders on the floor and forgets the other 65 conversations.

 Set the expectation before the show: if you talked to someone, you scanned them. No exceptions.

### 8. Write and Submit Orders on the Floor — Not at Midnight

 The order written during the appointment is the order that ships. The order written in a hotel room at 11pm — reconstructed from notes, annotated PDFs, and memory — is the order with errors.

 RepSparkGo is built for the show floor. Reps open the app, pull up the buyer's account, build the order against live inventory, and submit it before the buyer walks away. The buyer gets a confirmation. The order is in the system. The rep moves to the next appointment without a stack of handwritten notes to process later.

 For the PGA Show in Orlando, where the show floor is enormous and appointments run back-to-back, the ability to close and submit orders in real time is the difference between leaving with committed business and leaving with a follow-up list.

### 9. Run a Daily Team Debrief — 10 Minutes, Every Day

 Trade shows run three to five days. What's working on day one is not always what's working on day three. The product getting the most attention at the PGA Buying Summit might not be the product you led with in your pre-show presentations.

 Every morning before the floor opens, spend 10 minutes with your team. Which styles are getting the most interest? Which show specials are closing orders and which aren't landing? Which accounts showed up for appointments and which didn't, and what's the follow-up plan?

 The brands that adjust mid-show — that move product forward in the display based on what's resonating, that refine the pitch based on what buyers keep asking — close more business over the course of the show than the brands that run the same presentation every day regardless of what the floor is telling them.

## After the Show

### 10. Execute Follow-Up Within 48 Hours — Not 10 Days

 The window between when a buyer leaves a trade show and when they stop thinking about your brand is shorter than most brands assume. By the time your rep follows up a week later, the buyer has been back to their store, processed other orders, and moved on to the next thing on their list.

 Within 48 hours of the show closing, every badge scan should receive an outreach. Segment them: buyers who placed orders get a confirmation and a thank-you. Buyers who were interested but didn't order get a follow-up with your event microsite link and a specific reason to act now. Buyers who were cold contacts get a lighter touch with a catalog link and a clear next step.

 This is where the badge scanner data, your RepSpark account list, and your CRM come together. The follow-up is not a generic blast — it's a segmented sequence built on what you know about each conversation from the show floor.

 The brands that follow up fast and follow up specifically are the ones that convert the conversations that didn't close on the floor into orders that arrive two weeks later.

## The Bottom Line

 The PGA Buying Summit and the PGA Show in Orlando are the two highest-leverage selling moments in the golf wholesale calendar. The brands that treat them as events to attend leave with a stack of business cards. The brands that treat them as campaigns — with preparation, tools, and follow-through — leave with committed orders, new accounts, and a rep team that knows how to sell.

 RepSpark is the platform your team should be writing orders on before they walk onto the show floor. If your reps aren't set up and trained before the show opens, you're leaving orders on the table that a competitor with a better-prepared team will close instead.
