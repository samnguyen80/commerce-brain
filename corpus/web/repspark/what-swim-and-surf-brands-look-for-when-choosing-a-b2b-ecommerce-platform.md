# What Swim and Surf Brands Look for When Choosing a B2B Ecommerce Platform 

 
 
 
 by Tim McLain on August 28, 2026
 

 
 Swim and surf wholesale runs on a clock that most categories never feel.

 A print collection has one season. If a buyer does not commit during pre-book, that print does not get a second chance, because next year it is a different print. Miss the window and the inventory becomes a markdown problem, not a sales problem.

 That compression changes what matters in a B2B platform. Speed and visual merchandising stop being nice features and start being the whole game.

 Here is what brands in this category actually evaluate.

## They look for a catalog that sells the color story

 In most apparel categories, the product is the silhouette and color is a variation. In swim, the print is the product. A buyer choosing between eleven prints in the same cut is making a merchandising decision about their floor, not a size decision.

 That means a flat product grid fails. Buyers need to see the collection the way it will hang, grouped by color story, so they can build a cohesive assortment rather than picking SKUs in isolation.

 RepSpark's 2026 State of Wholesale report found that 95% of retailers are most influenced by lifestyle and brand imagery, ahead of detailed specs at 62%. In swim and surf that gap is wider still, because the imagery is doing the selling.

 L*Space, the California swim and resort brand, built Virtual Showrooms for their basics and for each color story so buyers could understand the full product mix as the line grew. Combined with digital assortments and catalogs, that approach helped drive B2B pre-book order growth of 214% year over year, with average order value up 28%.

## They look for pre-book buyers can drive themselves

 The pre-book window is short and it overlaps with trade shows, travel, and a sales team that cannot be everywhere.

 The brands that win this window let buyers place their own pre-books, then loop the rep in rather than gating on them. L*Space wanted exactly this. Select buyers could process their own pre-books, and reps received alerts when an order was submitted so they could review it, approve it, and look for opportunities to round out the assortment.

 That is the right model. The rep stops being a bottleneck and becomes an editor. The buyer works at midnight if that is when they have time, and the order still gets a professional set of eyes before it goes to the ERP.

 Ask any vendor whether buyer self-service pre-book is standard, and whether rep notification and approval come with it or arrive as a custom build.

## They look for size and separates handled natively

 Swim breaks the standard size grid in ways general platforms rarely anticipate.

 Tops and bottoms sell separately and in different sizes. Styles are designed to be mixed and matched. Cup sizing, long torso options, and boardshort waist runs all coexist in one line. Wetsuits carry their own dimension entirely.

 A platform has to model this at the variant level and let a buyer enter quantities across the whole grid in one pass. RepSpark structures product data at the Style, Color, and Size level with parent-child architecture, enabling matrix-style ordering built for entering bulk quantities quickly. Configurable product uniqueness rules matter here too, because a print that returns next season under the same style number will wreck your reporting if the model does not account for it.

## They look for seasons that reflect how they actually sell

 Swim does not run on Spring and Fall. It runs on a main season, a resort and cruise calendar that starts before the main season ends, and a closeout cycle that has to stay separate from both.

 RepSpark supports seasonal segmentation including Spring, Fall, Resort, and Limited Releases, and lets seasons work either as a structural attribute in the product model or as a filterable merchandising dimension depending on how the ERP is configured. Pre-book, at-once, and in-season ordering all run in the same system, with future ship date enforcement on pre-book, real-time availability on at-once, allocation controls, and rules that keep closeouts out of pre-book eligibility.

 That last rule sounds minor. Ask any customer service team that has spent a week unwinding closeout units booked into a future season.

## They look for territory and currency segmentation

 Swim and surf brands go international earlier than most categories, because the season never ends somewhere.

 That creates a real requirement. Different territories need different catalogs, different assortments, different pricing, and different currencies, without running three separate systems. L*Space established multiple catalogs and product assortments across territories. TYR, founded in Huntington Beach in 1985 for the competitive swim market, was managing a global retailer network across multiple currencies and retailer strategies through several disconnected order entry and inventory systems.

 RepSpark consolidated TYR onto one platform through API and ERP integration, gave reps and independent retailers self-service access through a custom-branded portal, and added KPI reporting on best-selling trends to guide assortment and retail buys. TYR saw 160% growth in independent retail users and 437% growth in B2B sales volume. Mary Sisson summed it up. "RepSpark has been an excellent tool for growing wholesale."

 International momentum is real across the platform. RepSpark saw 177.4% year over year growth in international orders in 2025, rising to 10.1% of total platform volume from 4.4% the year before.

## They look for reorder speed above almost everything

 In a short season, the reorder is where the margin lives. A print that sells through in three weeks is only a win if the shop can get more before the season turns.

 That requires accurate availability and an ordering flow a small shop can complete in minutes. RepSpark keeps the interface fast using high-frequency optimized inventory batches rather than hammering the ERP with continuous live queries, then runs a targeted validation at checkout so nothing oversells. Buyers can also check inventory without starting an order.

 The market is moving this direction hard. Reorders across the RepSpark platform grew 32.8% in 2025 over 2024, as retailers shifted toward smaller and more frequent buys rather than committing everything up front.

## They look for team, event, and shop program storefronts

 Competitive swim brings clubs, teams, and aquatics programs. Surf brings contests, shop events, and ambassador programs. Both need a temporary storefront with its own assortment and its own rules, and neither should require reconfiguring the main B2B portal.

 RepSpark handles this with microsites, which are event-based storefronts carrying their own assortments, pricing logic, branding, and inventory. Order processing settings can split orders based on whether items need decoration, which matters for team suits and caps. Where logos are involved, Insignia puts artwork upload, placement selection, and mockup preview inside the checkout flow rather than in an email chain.

## The evaluation question that cuts through

 Bring your real line to the demo. Load a full print collection with your actual color stories, tops and bottoms sized separately, and ask a rep to build a territory-specific pre-book while you watch.

 Then ask three things. Can a buyer complete this alone. Where does the closeout rule live. What happens when the same print returns next season.

 Vendors who have solved swim will answer immediately. Everyone else will start a sentence with the word "configurable."
