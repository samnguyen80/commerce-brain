## How Most Wholesale Brands Still Manage Orders

 

 
 
 
 
 

 

 
 


 
 


 

 


 

 

 

 
 
 

 
 
 
 



 
 
 

 
 
 Wholesale brands sit at the center of a supply chain that involves sales reps, retail buyers, inventory managers, and fulfillment teams. Coordinating all of them takes a system. For most brands, that system is a patchwork of tools that were never designed to work together: spreadsheets, phone calls, handwritten order forms, emailed PDFs, and fax machines that are somehow still running in 2026.

 This is not a fringe situation. Studies consistently show that a significant share of B2B trade, including wholesale apparel, outdoor, golf, and lifestyle brands, still runs on manual processes. Sales reps carry printed lookbooks and order forms to trade shows. Retailers place reorders by calling into the brand's customer service line. Order data gets typed by hand into an ERP system by someone in an office who was not at the sales meeting.

### The Old Wholesale Order Workflow, Step by Step

 For brands that have not yet adopted a B2B wholesale ecommerce platform, a typical seasonal order cycle looks like this:

 A sales rep visits a retail account or sets up at a trade show. They carry a physical or PDF line sheet showing the season's styles, colorways, and wholesale pricing. The buyer flips through it, makes selections, and the rep writes the order on a paper form or, at best, a shared spreadsheet template. If the rep is at the trade show, they may be writing dozens of orders across two or three days with no connection to the home office.

 The rep returns home with a folder of handwritten orders or a tablet full of spreadsheet files. Someone at the brand, often in customer service or sales operations, re-enters each order by hand into the ERP or fulfillment system. During this re-entry window, which may take days or even weeks after a major trade show, the brand has no reliable picture of what has been sold, what inventory has been committed, or which accounts placed orders at all.

 Retail buyers who did not see a rep at a show have limited options for placing new orders or reorders. Many call the brand's customer service line and read their order over the phone. Others email a spreadsheet. Some fill out a fax form. In the best cases, a brand has a static PDF order form on their website. But none of these methods give the buyer a branded, searchable catalog. None of them confirm availability at the time of order placement. And none of them eliminate the manual re-entry step on the brand's side.

### Why Brands Stay on Spreadsheets Longer Than They Should

 The honest answer is inertia. Spreadsheets are free, familiar, and flexible. A rep who has used the same order template for five years sees no immediate cost to keeping it. The brand's customer service team has a re-entry workflow that works well enough. The ERP is getting fed. Orders are shipping.

 What does not show up on any spreadsheet is the cost of the system itself: the orders that never made it in because the reorder process was too hard, the retailer relationships that eroded because follow-up was inconsistent, the inventory that was oversold because two reps wrote orders against the same stock without knowing it, or the sales ops headcount that exists entirely to turn paper into data.

 Modern B2B wholesale ecommerce platforms eliminate all of those costs. The brands that have switched are not just more efficient; they are selling more, at higher average order values, with fewer errors and lower operational overhead. The brands still on spreadsheets are competing against them with one hand tied behind their back.

## The Real Cost of Spreadsheet-Based Order Management

 

 
 
 
 
 

 

 
 


 
 


 

 


 

 

 

 
 
 

 
 
 
 



 
 
 

 
 
 When wholesale teams talk about the cost of their current system, they usually mean the direct cost: the software they are paying for, the staff hours they are spending, the trade show booth fees. The indirect costs are harder to see but often far larger.

### Overselling and Inventory Errors

 When orders are written by multiple reps with no shared view of available inventory, overselling is inevitable. Rep A writes a 200-unit order for a green polo at a trade show in Las Vegas. Rep B writes a 150-unit order for the same SKU at a show in New York. The brand has 250 units in stock. Both orders are submitted the same day. Someone has to make a phone call, apologize, and partially fulfill at least one account.

 That phone call has a real cost: damaged trust with a retail buyer, potential cancellation of other styles on the same order, and a customer service hour spent managing the fallout. When this happens repeatedly, retailers stop trusting the brand's inventory and start placing smaller, hedged orders to protect themselves from partial fulfillment. Average order values decline. Reorder frequency drops.

### Manual Re-Entry Errors and Delays

 Human re-entry of order data introduces errors at every step. A handwritten order that is illegible costs time to decode. A style number transposed by one digit ships the wrong product. A wholesale price entered incorrectly creates invoice disputes. An order that takes two weeks to process after a trade show may miss the retailer's floor set window entirely, causing the whole order to be cancelled.

 Re-entry labor is also not cheap. Brands with active trade show calendars and dozens of sales reps may have one or more full-time staff members whose primary job is turning order paperwork into ERP entries. That headcount cost is entirely eliminated by a platform that connects order capture to the ERP directly.

### Missed Reorders and Lost Revenue

 The reorder is where most wholesale brands leave the most money on the table. A retailer sells through a style faster than expected. They want to reorder. If the process for doing so requires a phone call during business hours, a spreadsheet submission, or waiting for their sales rep to call them back, many will simply move on. The style goes out of stock at retail. The brand loses the reorder revenue. And the retailer's buyer starts wondering whether this brand is worth the hassle of carrying.

 Brands running on modern B2B platforms report reorder rates that are dramatically higher than before their switch. The mechanism is simple: when reordering is as easy as logging into a portal and clicking add to order, retailers do it. When it requires effort, they often do not.

### Rep Productivity Wasted on Administration

 Sales reps are paid to sell. On a manual system, a significant share of their time goes to non-selling activities: preparing order forms before a show, transcribing orders after a show, following up with accounts who never received a confirmation, and handling disputes caused by errors in the order entry process. A rep who spends 30 percent of their time on order administration is 30 percent less productive as a seller than they could be.

 Modern wholesale platforms move all of that administration off the rep's plate. Order confirmation is automatic. Inventory availability is checked at the moment of order placement. The ERP receives the order without anyone typing it in. The rep goes to the next account.

### Limited Visibility Into What Is Actually Selling

 A brand running on spreadsheets has limited ability to see, in aggregate, what is selling, which accounts are most active, or where reorder opportunities exist. Analytics require exporting spreadsheets, stitching data together manually, and waiting for someone to build a report. By the time the data is ready, the opportunity has passed.

 Modern wholesale platforms surface this data continuously, without export or manual assembly. Brand managers can see which styles are trending in orders, which accounts have not placed a reorder, and which reps are most productive, all from a single dashboard.

## What Modern Wholesale Order Management Looks Like

 

 
 
 
 
 

 

 
 


 
 


 

 


 

 

 

 
 
 

 
 
 
 



 
 
 

 
 
 A modern B2B wholesale ecommerce platform does not just digitize the old order form. It replaces the entire workflow, from how buyers discover a brand's line to how orders flow into fulfillment, with a system designed for the way wholesale actually works.

### A Branded Buyer Portal That Is Always Open

 The foundational shift is from order-on-request to order-on-demand. In a modern platform, a brand's retail accounts can log in to a private, branded buyer portal at any time, browse the full seasonal catalog, check available inventory, and place orders without calling anyone. The portal enforces the brand's pricing tiers, minimum order quantities, and order rules automatically. It is the brand's wholesale storefront, open 24 hours a day, seven days a week.

 This alone changes the economics of reordering. When reordering requires no phone call and no wait for a callback, reorder frequency increases. When buyers can place an order at 10 PM from their home office after closing time, some of them will. Those orders would not have happened under the old system.

### Digital Catalogs That Replace PDF Line Sheets

 Modern platforms provide interactive digital catalogs that replace the static PDF line sheet. A digital catalog is searchable, filterable by category or colorway, linked directly to order entry, and always current. A rep who previews a new style to a buyer does not hand over a printed lookbook; they share a link to the brand's digital showroom where the buyer can immediately start building an order.

 Digital line sheets can also be customized per account, showing only the styles that are relevant to a given buyer and reflecting that buyer's negotiated pricing. An independent sales rep managing multiple brand lines can maintain separate, fully branded digital showrooms for each brand, all from a single login.

### Rep Order-Writing Tools for Trade Shows and In-Store Visits

 Modern platforms serve both the buyer who wants to self-serve and the rep who is writing orders face-to-face with a retail account. At a trade show or in-store appointment, a rep opens the platform on a tablet or laptop, pulls up the brand's catalog, and writes the order with the buyer in the room. The order is immediately visible to the brand's team. No order forms. No re-entry. No phone call when the show is over.

 This trade show order-writing capability extends to group buying events where a brand hosts multiple retail buyers. An order written in the morning is reflected in available inventory counts by the afternoon. Overselling becomes structurally impossible because every order is written against the same live available-to-sell pool.

### ERP Integration That Eliminates Re-Entry

 The most operationally significant capability of a modern wholesale platform is its integration with the brand's ERP system. When an order is placed in the platform, by a buyer self-serving in the portal or by a rep writing it at a trade show, it flows directly into the ERP without manual re-entry. Product data flows out of the ERP into the platform automatically, keeping the catalog current without manual uploads.

 This integration collapses the gap between when an order is placed and when it is in the fulfillment system. It eliminates the re-entry staff cost. And it eliminates the re-entry error rate, because the data never passes through human hands between order capture and fulfillment processing.

### Analytics That Turn Orders Into Intelligence

 Every order placed on a modern platform generates data that is immediately visible to the brand. Which styles are moving fastest in pre-book orders? Which accounts have not placed a reorder in 90 days? Which reps wrote the most volume at the last trade show? What is the average order value by channel? This intelligence was previously available only to brands with a data team and a BI tool. Modern wholesale platforms surface it in dashboards that anyone on the team can read.

 Chapter IV
 
 

 
 
 
 

## Features to Look for in a Wholesale Platform

 

 
 
 
 
 

 

 
 


 
 


 

 


 

 

 

 
 
 

 
 
 
 



 
 
 

 
 
 Not all B2B wholesale platforms are built for the same kind of brand. Some are designed primarily for large, multi-category retailers. Others are optimized for brands in a single vertical. Some are strong on marketplace discovery and weak on operational tools. When evaluating platforms, here are the capabilities that matter most for a wholesale brand moving off spreadsheets.

### Branded Buyer Portal With Gated Access

 The platform should support a fully branded buyer portal that looks like the brand's own environment, not a generic marketplace. Access should be gated and account-specific, with the ability to set different pricing tiers, assortments, and terms for different buyer segments. Buyers should be able to browse, build orders, and submit without any friction from generic or unbranded interfaces.

### Digital Catalog and Line Sheet Tools

 The platform should support interactive digital catalogs and the ability to create and share customized line sheets by account or account segment. Line sheets should be shareable via link, not downloadable only as PDFs, so that the buyer experience is native to the platform and orders initiated from a line sheet flow directly into the order system.

### Rep Order-Writing for Trade Shows and Field Calls

 If the brand uses sales reps in the field or at trade shows, the platform must support rep order-writing on a tablet or laptop. The rep should be able to write an order in the presence of the buyer, and that order should appear in the brand's system in real time. The interface should be fast enough to use in a busy trade show environment and stable enough to function under variable connectivity conditions.

### Inventory Availability at Order Placement

 The platform should check available inventory before an order is confirmed, not after. This is what prevents overselling. A buyer should be able to see, at the style and size level, whether the quantity they are requesting is available to sell. If stock is limited, the platform should communicate that clearly so the buyer can adjust their order rather than discovering a partial fulfillment weeks later.

### ERP and Commerce Integrations

 For the platform to eliminate manual re-entry, it must integrate directly with the brand's ERP system. The most common ERP integrations for wholesale brands are NetSuite and ApparelMagic. Brands that also operate a direct-to-consumer channel should look for platforms that integrate with Shopify or similar commerce systems, so that inventory is shared across wholesale and DTC without manual reconciliation.

### Retailer Discovery or Marketplace Network

 Beyond servicing existing accounts, the best platforms provide a way for brands to be discovered by retail buyers they have not yet reached. This is a meaningful distinction from a standalone buyer portal, which only serves accounts the brand already knows about. A platform with an embedded retailer marketplace allows the brand to grow its wholesale account base without adding headcount.

### Analytics and Reporting

 At minimum, the platform should provide rep performance reporting, account activity tracking, style-level order volume, and reorder frequency data. Brands with more sophisticated needs should look for platforms that surface this data in real time and allow filtering by season, channel, rep, or account segment without requiring a data export.

## RepSpark vs. NuOrder, Elastic Suite, and JOOR

### RepSpark vs. NuOrder

 NuOrder is a broadly distributed wholesale platform used across a wide range of categories, including apparel, home goods, and consumer products. Its buyer marketplace includes buyers from major retailers alongside independent specialty accounts. NuOrder's strength is network scale across categories.

 RepSpark's strength is depth in specialty categories, particularly apparel, golf, outdoor, surf, and lifestyle, with a retailer network heavily concentrated in the specialty retail, green grass, resort, and lifestyle channels that matter most to brands in those categories. RepSpark is the most-used wholesale ordering platform in the green grass channel, with more than 9,000 green grass facilities actively ordering through the platform. For a golf or outdoor brand, that channel concentration is more valuable than broad multi-category scale.

 RepSpark also combines the buyer portal, digital showroom, and rep order-writing tool into a single unified platform. Brands that need all three capabilities do not have to stitch together separate tools or manage separate logins.

### RepSpark vs. Elastic Suite

 Elastic Suite is a B2B wholesale platform with a strength in catalog presentation and assortment planning tools. It is used by brands in outdoor, sporting goods, and hardgoods categories and by some larger apparel brands. Elastic's assortment planning capability is more developed than most competitors.

 RepSpark's advantage is in the combination of trade show order-writing, marketplace retailer discovery, and ERP integration. For brands that need to write orders at trade shows, surface their line to new independent retail accounts, and connect orders directly to NetSuite or ApparelMagic without middleware, RepSpark is a more complete operational solution.

### RepSpark vs. JOOR

 JOOR is a wholesale marketplace platform weighted toward the luxury, contemporary fashion, and department store buyer segments. Its marketplace is strongest for brands pursuing placement in upscale department stores and international fashion retailers. JOOR's global marketplace reach in luxury and contemporary fashion is a genuine strength for brands in that segment.

 For specialty brands in apparel, golf, outdoor, surf, and lifestyle categories whose accounts are primarily independent specialty retailers, green grass pro shops, and regional specialty chains, JOOR's buyer network is a less relevant fit.  RepSpark's most distinctive capability is the combination of all wholesale touchpoints inside a single platform: the buyer portal for self-service reorders, the Virtual Showroom for digital-first selling, FieldShow for trade show and in-person order writing, RepSpark Community for retailer discovery and acquisition, and native ERP integrations that eliminate manual re-entry.  RepSpark is also the only wholesale platform in which the PGA of America has established a strategic alignment , reflecting RepSpark's position as the commerce infrastructure for golf brands selling into the green grass channel. For brands in the golf category, that alignment means the platform most pro shops are already using is also the platform most golf brands are using to manage their wholesale business.

 Chapter VII
 
 

 
 
 
 

## How to Make the Switch: Moving Off Spreadsheets

 

 
 
 
 
 

 

 
 


 
 


 

 


 

 

 

 
 
 

 
 
 
 



 
 
 

 
 
 Moving from spreadsheet-based order management to a modern wholesale platform does not require a multi-year implementation. RepSpark brands typically go live in weeks. The transition is smoother when it follows a logical sequence.

### Step 1: Audit the Current Workflow

 Before evaluating any platform, map how orders actually move through the brand today. Where do orders originate? Which reps, channels, and buyer segments generate the most volume? Where are the most common errors? How many staff hours per week go to re-entry, follow-up, and error correction? This audit defines what a modern platform needs to replace and sets the baseline for measuring improvement.

### Step 2: Prioritize Your Integration Requirements

 The ERP integration is the most operationally significant part of a platform switch. Identify which ERP the brand is running, which data needs to flow in which direction, and what the acceptable lag is between an order placed in the platform and an order appearing in the ERP. For most brands using NetSuite or ApparelMagic, RepSpark's native integrations handle this without custom development.

### Step 3: Start With One Channel

 Rather than switching all order channels simultaneously, most brands have the smoothest transitions when they start with a single channel. The buyer portal for direct retailer self-service is usually the easiest starting point, because it does not require retraining the sales rep team. Once the portal is live and account activation is underway, the brand can layer in the rep order-writing tool and digital showroom capabilities.

### Step 4: Activate Your Retail Accounts

 Account activation is the step that determines how quickly the brand sees the return on its platform investment. Most brands underestimate what it takes to move retail buyers from their existing ordering habits to a new portal. A proactive activation campaign, combining a personal email from the rep to each account, a short explainer on how to place their first portal order, and a small incentive for early adopters, typically achieves activation rates far higher than a passive rollout.

### Step 5: Enable RepSpark Community for New Account Discovery

 Once the platform is live for existing accounts, enabling the brand's listing on RepSpark Community opens the door to inbound discovery from the 100,000+ active retailers on the network. Brands that activate Community within the first quarter of their RepSpark launch typically see their first new Community-sourced accounts within 30 days of going live.

### Step 6: Measure and Iterate

 With order data flowing through the platform, the brand now has visibility it never had under the spreadsheet system. Track portal adoption by account, reorder frequency before and after launch, average order value by channel, and rep order-writing volume at trade shows. Use RepSpark's analytics to identify which accounts are not yet active and have the rep team reach out personally to those accounts. The brands that see the fastest results are the ones that treat the platform as a living sales tool, not a set-and-forget software implementation.

## Glossary of B2B Wholesale Order Management Terms & FAQ

 

 
 
 
 
 

 

 
 


 
 


 

 


 

 

 

 
 
 

 
 
 
 



 
 
 

 
 
 
 Available-to-Sell (ATS) 
 The quantity of a given SKU that is uncommitted and available for new orders at a given moment. ATS is the inventory figure that should be checked at the time of order placement to prevent overselling. 
 B2B Wholesale Ecommerce Platform

 Buyer Portal

 Digital Line Sheet 
 An interactive, shareable document presenting curated product selections with imagery, pricing, and direct links to order entry. Digital line sheets replace the emailed PDF as the standard pre-market and pre-show communication tool. 
 Digital Showroom 
 A branded, interactive online environment where a wholesale brand presents its seasonal collection to retail buyers. A digital showroom is transactional, meaning buyers can place orders directly inside it, as distinct from a static lookbook or PDF. 
 ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) 
 A software system used to manage a business's core operations, including inventory, order management, fulfillment, and accounting. Common ERPs used by wholesale brands include NetSuite and ApparelMagic. Integration between a wholesale platform and the ERP eliminates manual re-entry of order data.

 RepSpark's rep order-writing tool designed for use at trade shows, regional events, and in-store appointments. Reps write orders on a tablet or laptop in real time, and those orders are immediately visible to the brand's home office and reflected in available-to-sell inventory counts.

 Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ) 
 The smallest order quantity a brand will accept for a given product, style, or order overall. Wholesale platforms can enforce MOQ rules automatically at checkout, eliminating the need for manual review of each order before processing. 
 Overselling 
 Accepting orders for more inventory than is available to ship. Overselling occurs when multiple order channels, reps, or buyers commit the same stock without a shared real-time view of available inventory. It results in partial fulfillment, retailer dissatisfaction, and damaged brand relationships. 
 Pre-Book 
 Orders placed in advance of a season's production or delivery window, allowing a brand to plan production quantities based on committed demand. Pre-book orders are typically placed at trade shows or through digital showrooms before the season begins.

 The systems and processes a brand uses to capture, validate, fulfill, and track orders from retail buyers. Modern wholesale order management platforms replace manual spreadsheet, fax, and email order workflows with automated, integrated digital tools. 
 

## FAQs: Modern Wholesale Order Management

 

### What is the biggest problem with managing wholesale orders in spreadsheets?

 The biggest operational problem is the absence of a shared, live view of available inventory. When reps in different locations write orders simultaneously with no connection to a central inventory system, overselling is inevitable. The second biggest problem is manual re-entry: order data written by a rep has to be typed by someone into the ERP before it enters the fulfillment system, introducing errors and delays that cost brands money and retailer relationships. Modern B2B wholesale platforms solve both problems by connecting order capture directly to inventory availability and ERP systems.

 

### How long does it take to switch from spreadsheets to RepSpark?

 Most RepSpark brands go live within a few weeks of starting onboarding, not months. The implementation timeline depends on the complexity of the ERP integration and the number of retail accounts that need to be activated. RepSpark's onboarding team manages the ERP connection and provides activation support for retail accounts. Brands with native NetSuite or ApparelMagic integrations typically have the fastest go-live timelines.

 

### Can independent sales reps use RepSpark if they represent multiple brands?

 Yes. Independent sales reps can manage multiple brand lines from a single RepSpark login. Each brand has its own fully branded digital showroom and buyer portal, independently configured with that brand's catalog, pricing, and order rules. A rep writing orders at a trade show can move between brands without switching systems or logging in and out of separate accounts.

 

### Does RepSpark work for small wholesale brands, or is it built for large ones?

 RepSpark serves wholesale brands at every stage, from brands launching their first wholesale season to established brands with hundreds of retail accounts and national rep networks. The buyer portal and digital showroom capabilities are immediately useful for a small brand that wants to enable retail buyers to place orders without calling the founder. RepSpark Community is a particularly valuable channel for smaller brands that do not yet have large rep networks, because it gives them access to an active base of 100,000+ retailers from day one.

 

### What ERP systems does RepSpark integrate with?

 RepSpark integrates natively with NetSuite, ApparelMagic, and Shopify. Additional integrations are available for other ERP and commerce systems. The integration with the brand's ERP is what eliminates manual re-entry of order data, so it is typically the first integration configured during onboarding.

 

### How does RepSpark prevent overselling?

 RepSpark syncs against available inventory before every order is confirmed, checking available-to-sell quantities at the style and size level before the order is placed. When a rep writes an order at a trade show, the available-to-sell count updates immediately so that another rep cannot commit the same stock to a different account. When a buyer places a self-service order in the portal, the same check happens before the order is submitted. This means every order that goes into RepSpark can be fulfilled as written.

 

### What is RepSpark Community, and how does it help brands find new retailers?

 RepSpark Community is the wholesale retailer marketplace inside RepSpark, with more than 100,000 active retailers who can browse and request access to brands listed on the platform. Brands review and approve each access request before granting catalog access, so the brand maintains full control over its distribution. Once a retailer is approved, they are inside the RepSpark environment for all future ordering, with no platform migration required. For brands moving off spreadsheets, Community is a built-in new account acquisition channel that operates without adding rep headcount.
