# Why Vertical Focus Beats a One-Size-Fits-All Wholesale Platform 

 
 
 
 by Sawyer Frank on August 28, 2026
 

 
 Every general-purpose commerce platform can be configured to run golf wholesale. That is true, and it is also the most expensive sentence in B2B software.

 Configuration is not a one-time cost. It is a permanent one. Every rule you build yourself is a rule you maintain yourself, re-test after every upgrade, and re-explain to every new hire. Meanwhile the brand next door bought a platform where those rules were already the defaults, shipped in six months instead of eighteen, and spent the difference on product.

 That is the real argument for vertical focus. Not that horizontal platforms cannot do golf. That they have not already done it.

## What vertical focus actually means

 It is not a logo on a landing page or a case study in the right category. Vertical focus shows up in three specific places.

 Defaults. The out-of-the-box behavior matches how the industry already works. Pre-book, at-once, and in-season ordering are order types, not custom fields someone has to invent.

 Vocabulary. The system uses the words your team uses. RepSpark's own team puts it plainly, saying they understand what you mean when you use industry lingo like WIP date. That sounds small until you have sat through a requirements workshop explaining what a size run is to an implementation consultant billing hourly.

 Edge cases treated as the main case. In golf wholesale, decorated product, tournament orders, and a long tail of tiny accounts are not edge cases. They are Tuesday. A platform built for general B2B treats each one as a special project.

## 5 places golf breaks a horizontal platform

 The green grass long tail. Golf brands do not sell to fifty large retailers. They sell to hundreds or thousands of pro shops, private clubs, resorts, and off-course specialty stores, most of them ordering small quantities frequently. Primo Golf scaled past 1,000 green grass accounts on RepSpark. That account structure punishes any platform that prices per retailer, requires a rep to touch every order, or assumes a buyer will tolerate a slow portal.

 RepSpark includes unlimited retailer accounts in its subscription with no per-customer licensing fees and no percentage charge on gross merchandise volume. That is not a discount. It is a design decision that only makes sense if you assume the long tail is the business.

 Tournament and event ordering. A member-guest, a corporate outing, or a charity scramble does not fit your seasonal calendar. It needs its own storefront, its own product set, its own ordering window, and it needs to exist for three weeks. Building that on a general platform means either a custom project per event or telling the shop no.

 RepSpark treats it as a feature. Microsites are branded storefronts separate from the main B2B site, with configurable ordering limits, package building, flat-rate shipping, order splitting based on whether an item needs decoration, and bulk user invites. Straight Down ran more than 70 microsites in 2023 and averaged over $11,000 in revenue per event. Robert Ogden described why they mattered. "Microsites allowed us to keep serving customers even when golf shops were closed." Across the platform, 38 brands use microsites, moving roughly $16 million annually at about $15,000 average event revenue.

 Olu-Kai found the same thing outside tournaments. Hillary Wiebe said, "Microsites have been a way for us to capture more business and keep relationships direct."

 Decoration as a first-class order attribute. Golf runs on logos. A club crest on a left chest, a sponsor mark on a sleeve, a foursome's initials. On a general platform, that is usually an email thread, a PDF proof, and three approvals sitting outside the order.

 RepSpark's Insignia module puts artwork upload, placement selection, and mockup preview inside the checkout flow, and decoration attributes ride on the order line itself. The platform also integrates with decoration software such as EmbiTrak. johnnie-O grew custom embroidery orders more than 500% and B2B order volume 744%. Katie Daly-Benjamin, their COO, said, "We couldn't operate without RepSpark."

 Licensing and royalties. Collegiate, resort, and club licensing carry rules about approved logos, approved products, approved color combinations, and royalty rates that typically run between 5% and 12% per sale. Getting that wrong is not an inconvenience, it is a contract breach.

 RepSpark's licensing solution narrows product options to licensor-approved combinations at order time and calculates royalties automatically. Fifty-seven brands use it. A horizontal platform can be made to do this. It will be a custom build, and it will be yours to maintain forever.

 Customer types that are not retail tiers. Pro shops, private clubs, resorts, off-course retailers, and specialty accounts each need different pricing, terms, allocation eligibility, and assortment visibility. RepSpark configures these through the customer master with pricing logic synchronized from the ERP and enforced at the account level. Generic platforms model customer groups. Golf needs customer types with real behavioral differences behind them.

## The market backing this up

 Golf is not a niche worth a polite nod. It is one of the healthiest categories in specialty retail.

 The National Golf Foundation reported total participation at a record 48.1 million in 2025, up 2% over 2024 and up 55% over the past decade. Off-course participation reached 37.9 million and on-course reached 29.1 million. New demographics are driving it, with female participation up 46% since 2019 to 8.1 million and participation among people of color up 61% to 7.7 million.

 RepSpark's own platform data tracks that growth. The number of retailers identifying as selling golf products grew 41.8% in 2025. According to a 2025 Golf Merchandisers Survey, 80% of golf retailers use RepSpark in their daily operations.

 That last number is the part that compounds. When most of the buyers in a category already log into the same platform, joining it is not an implementation, it is a distribution decision. Turtleson reached more than 40,000 retailers through RepSpark's Community, opened over 200 new shopping relationships, and generated more than $114,000 in Community-driven revenue. Andy Plate, their President, put it directly. "We felt like we had to be in the Community to be relevant."

 A horizontal platform gives you a website. A vertical platform with real density in your category gives you a market.

## What focus lets you build

 Because RepSpark only has to solve wholesale for apparel, golf, outdoor, and tactical brands, its architecture reflects choices a general platform cannot make.

 Product data is structured at the Style, Color, and Size level with parent-child architecture, enabling matrix-style ordering built for entering bulk quantities across a full size run rather than clicking into variants one at a time.

 The cart enforces wholesale validation before checkout, including minimum order thresholds, pre-pack requirements, restricted SKU rules, closeout exclusions, allocation limits, and customer-specific eligibility.

 Inventory takes a pragmatic approach. Rather than hammering your ERP with continuous live queries, RepSpark uses high-frequency optimized batches to keep the interface fast, then runs a targeted validation at the moment of checkout. Buyers never oversell, and IT never gets a stability problem it did not ask for.

 Integration runs on a modern API-driven architecture with published documentation at developers.repspark.com, support for REST APIs, webhooks, and batch, and three integration models so you choose who owns the change costs. Inventory and orders sync with the ERP every 15 minutes.

 RepSpark Flow, the current platform release, rebuilt these workflows around how buyers actually move through ordering rather than legacy assumptions, and added AI-powered order insights that surface gaps and opportunities while an order is being built.

 The results show up across categories. TYR grew B2B sales volume 437% and independent retail users 160%. Mary Sisson said, "RepSpark has been an excellent tool for growing wholesale." Nexbelt cut order processing from ten minutes to one, an 80% reduction, with Seth Halliwill noting, "I don't have to talk to a co-worker to know when things are arriving. I just look it up in RepSpark." NAOT added 400 new retailers and grew revenue 15% in three months. EssilorLuxottica modernized wholesale for Oakley and Ray-Ban, with Ewan King describing how "through branded customer microsites, the platform extends selling beyond traditional appointments, enabling always-on access to our assortments." 5.11 Tactical unified global operations across more than 1,600 B2B buyers.

## When a horizontal platform is the better choice

 Vertical focus is a real tradeoff, and pretending otherwise would be the kind of overselling worth avoiding.

 If your wholesale business is a small share of a much larger direct-to-consumer operation and you want one system for everything, a general platform may cost less in total even after configuration.

 If you need a large third-party app ecosystem, a deep pool of developers who already know the stack, or heavy customization outside wholesale workflows, horizontal platforms have the advantage.

 If your product has no variants, no decoration, no seasonal ordering windows, and no licensing, most of what makes a vertical platform valuable does not apply to you.

 And any specialized vendor concentrates risk. You are betting on one company's roadmap in one category. Ask about financial stability and long-term commitment before you sign, which is a fair question to put to RepSpark alongside everyone else.

## The question worth asking

 On your next evaluation, skip the feature checklist and ask one thing. How many customers do you have that look exactly like us, and can I talk to three of them.

 A horizontal platform will describe how flexible it is. A vertical platform will hand you a list.

The company also deepened its commitment to golf specifically through a partnership with the Association of Golf Merchandisers, funding five Certified Retail Manager scholarships for the merchandisers running the shops our brands sell to.

 Flexibility is worth something. Being already built for your business is worth more.
