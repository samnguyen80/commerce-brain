## What is a headless ecommerce platform? 
 Headless ecommerce is becoming another commerce buzzword. But many people don’t know what it means to be headless — just that they need to be.
 The term headless ecommerce platform describes a type of ecommerce architecture that splits up a front end, or the “head” (whether it’s a desktop website, mobile app, or social channel) and the back end, where transactions are processed.
 Commercetools CEO is credited with coining the term headless commerce, and the company is reaping the benefits. Commercetools was recently valued at $1.9B . Other headless commerce companies are seeing similar fanfare as the need to enable outdated tech infrastructure increases.
 The buzz around headless ecommerce platforms is considerable, with many different types of businesses buying in. Amazon was built as a headless marketplace , while retail brands like Nike and Under Armour have also gone headless. More companies from other industries across the B2C and B2B spectrum are following suit .

### Understanding headless ecommerce architecture 
 Headless ecommerce architecture separates the front end of your site from the back end. The front end is what customers see — your storefront. The back end handles operations like inventory, orders, and payments.
 With headless architecture, these two parts work independently. Instead of being tightly connected, they communicate through APIs. This setup gives businesses more flexibility. You can design a custom storefront without being limited by the back-end system.
 Headless ecommerce is the foundation of composable commerce. Composable commerce allows businesses to create a tailored ecommerce system by combining the best tools and microservices for each function. For example, you might use one tool for inventory management, another for customer engagement, and a third for shipping. APIs connect these tools, so they work together seamlessly.
 
 By decoupling the front end and back end, headless platforms make composable commerce possible. This approach gives businesses the flexibility to innovate, scale, and adapt as their needs change.
 

## Why are companies using headless ecommerce platforms? 
 Headless tech proliferates due to fundamentally broken experiences like social commerce, mobile commerce, catalog syndication, multi-vendor, and marketplace commerce. Commerce tech incumbents are at least 20 years old and built on rigid architectures. For example, most of the major ecommerce platforms were built before social networks and smartphones existed. Organizations can’t innovate on a platform that lacks the agility to respond to the shift in buyer expectations.  
 On the storefront side, companies are locked into the limitations of the existing ecommerce platforms with frontends and template builders that don’t allow a differentiated customer experience. You will always be stuck at the pace of development of these platforms.
 On the infrastructure side, companies want to get all the different tools off the shelf and build an experience that is specific and custom. This is particularly interesting to large companies with an extensive stack because they can easily stick a new module into their existing stack.
 Many companies today look for a “composable architecture” where they can fashion their ideal customer experience but require a flexible backend to support their workflows to deliver that experience.
 You can use a headless strategy to have the best-in-breed app for your OMS, PIM , CMS, CRM — everything. Ideally, they should all be API (application programming interfaces) native , connect to each other, and run off a centralized data system.
 However, companies don’t necessarily want to create headless stacks by themselves. This can be a high-stakes investment, especially for a headless marketplace, which involves multiple vendors, each with their own inventory and orders. In response, tech startups, like Nautical , are responding with different headless solutions.
 

## Headless ecommerce vs. headless marketplaces 
 Headless ecommerce platforms are versatile. They provide the flexibility to design custom storefronts and integrate advanced tools. Whether you’re running a single-seller store or a multi-vendor marketplace, the right headless platform can meet your needs.
 Marketplaces, however, come with added complexities. They need features like vendor management, multi-vendor checkouts, payment splits, and more. A headless platform built for marketplaces needs to be able to support these advanced workflows.

## What are the benefits of headless ecommerce platforms? 
 Headless ecommerce platforms are the antidote to traditional systems. In this section, we cover four key benefits that show why businesses are choosing headless platforms.

### 1. Flexibility for custom storefronts 
 Off-the-shelf ecommerce platforms often come with rigid templates. These templates limit how a storefront can look or function. A headless ecommerce platform removes these restrictions. It separates the front end from the back end, allowing businesses to design a storefront to their liking.
 This flexibility is critical for personalization. At least 80% of consumers expect tailored shopping experiences. Headless platforms let you build marketplaces with unique experiences for each brand or vendor. 

### 2. Front-end changes can be deployed rapidly 
 Headless ecommerce platforms allow developers to make fast front-end changes. There’s no need to wait for compatibility checks or deal with legacy infrastructure. Updates go out quickly, letting businesses respond to customer needs or trends.
 For marketplace operators, this is a major advantage. They can experiment with new designs or features without long delays. Multi-vendor marketplaces benefit from this speed by staying agile in a competitive market.

### 3. Headless has better back-end integrations 
 Modern ecommerce relies on specialized tools. From managing customer data to storing product information, businesses use multiple systems to stay efficient. Traditional platforms often struggle with these integrations. They force businesses to rely on outdated, monolithic solutions.
 Headless platforms solve this. They use APIs to connect the front and back ends. This gives operators the freedom to choose the systems that work best for their needs. For marketplaces, this creates smooth operations across vendors and buyers.

### 4. Headless ecommerce platforms improve conversion 
 Page speed has a direct impact on conversions. According to a study by Portent , a site that loads in one second has a conversion rate three times higher than a site that loads in 5 seconds.Traditional platforms often require plugins or add-ons to handle new ecommerce features. Over time, this slows down the site and frustrates users.
 Headless ecommerce platforms avoid this problem. By decoupling the front and back ends, they deliver faster loading times and better performance. This is critical for marketplaces where large catalogs and high traffic can strain legacy systems. Faster sites not only improve user experience but also help with search engine rankings.

## How to choose the right headless ecommerce platform
 We&#x27;ve covered some of the best headless ecommerce platforms. But how do you choose? Here are the key factors to consider when evaluating your options.

### Business size and needs
 The size of your business impacts your platform choice. Smaller businesses may need simpler solutions to avoid unnecessary costs or complexity. Larger businesses or marketplaces often require scalability and advanced features.
 Think about your future needs too. For example, a company selling products across North America might plan to expand into Europe. This shift would require the platform to support multiple languages, currencies, and regional tax rules. A platform built to handle international operations will make this transition smoother and faster.

### Integration capabilities
 Headless ecommerce platforms need to integrate with the tools you already use. This might include logistics systems like ShipBob, manufacturing software like Katana, or ERP tools like NetSuite. A strong API-first approach means these systems can work together.
 Composable commerce relies on these integrations. Instead of using a single platform for everything, businesses can combine best-in-class tools to build a tailored system. For example, a marketplace might need an identity verification tool for vendors, advanced analytics for tracking performance, and dynamic shipping integrations to manage multiple warehouses. A headless platform should support these connections seamlessly. 

### Budget and pricing
 Cost is obviously a critical factor. Some platforms charge based on sales volume, while others have flat fees or tiered pricing. Be clear about what’s included. For example, one platform might charge extra for advanced reporting, while another includes it in the base price.
 Look for a headless ecommerce platform that balances cost with value. Don’t choose the cheapest option if it limits your ability to grow or operate effectively.

### Developer resources and support
 Headless platforms often require some level of technical expertise. If your team has developers, you’ll need a platform that provides clear documentation and tools. For instance, robust API documentation can speed up integration and customizations.
 If you don’t have developers, consider platforms that offer support or certified partners. For example, some platforms have dedicated partner networks for implementation.
